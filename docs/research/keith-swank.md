# Keith Swank Research

**Last verified:** November 2, 2026, America/Los_Angeles (November 3 UTC retrieval session).

**Purpose:** Standing background for coverage of Pierce County Sheriff Keith Swank, particularly his organizing for an anti-trans demonstration at the November 1, 2026 Seattle Reign match. Primary records take priority. This is an evidence dossier, not a finding that every complaint against him is true.

The report is dated November 25, 2024. Use this record for his election, not the county biography's search-index wording that incorrectly places the election in January 2025. [2] Source checked: [county biography](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/126/Meet-Your-Sheriff).

## 2. Seattle OPA case 2023OPA-0194: completed administrative findings

### Identity, date, and outcome

- **Primary identity bridge:** The CJTC's official folder for Swank case **2024-0012903** contains *1915 Swank, Keith_Redacted.pdf*. Its first-page factual summary identifies **former Captain Swank** and explicitly says **“Reference 2023OPA-0194.”** It describes an October 24, 2024 discipline-committee decision that, had he remained employed, the recommendation would have been serious discipline up to and including termination. This official preview was visually inspected. [10] Source: [CJTC-hosted agency report, p. 1](https://cjtc.app.box.com/v/2024-0012903-Swank/file/2129427992411).
- **Correct issue date:** The OPA PDF's printed issued date is **January 30, 2025**, despite its URL ending `ccs10-10-24.pdf`. Its author line is Deputy Director Bonnie Glenn on behalf of Director Gino Betts Jr., Office of Police Accountability. Cite the printed date, not a date inferred from the filename. [3] Source: [OPA summary, p. 1](https://www.seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/OPA/ClosedCaseSummaries/2023OPA-0194ccs10-10-24.pdf).
- **Three sustained findings:** The front-page Director's Findings table lists **Sustained** for bias-free policing (5.140), the personal-social-media restriction on speech negatively affecting the department's ability to serve the public (5.125), and professionalism (5.001). These are completed departmental administrative findings, not merely an unresolved complaint and not a criminal conviction. [3] Source: [OPA summary, p. 1](https://www.seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/OPA/ClosedCaseSummaries/2023OPA-0194ccs10-10-24.pdf).
- **Not fired:** The discipline entry reads **“Retired Prior to Proposed Discipline – Termination.”** OPA records his June 28, 2023 separation and an October 2023 interview request he declined. Do not write that Seattle fired him or imposed termination. [3] Source: [OPA summary, pp. 1 and 3](https://www.seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/OPA/ClosedCaseSummaries/2023OPA-0194ccs10-10-24.pdf).

### What OPA actually established

- **Anti-trans statements:** The summary reproduces “Transwomen are men” and statements portraying transgender people as “gender defiant” or “gender confused” and needing mental-health help. OPA concluded that the evidence established, more likely than not, prejudiced and derogatory social-media comments concerning gender, gender identity, political ideology, and race. Attribute that conclusion to OPA. [3] Source: [OPA summary, pp. 2 and 4](https://www.seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/OPA/ClosedCaseSummaries/2023OPA-0194ccs10-10-24.pdf).
- **Public trust:** In its social-media analysis, OPA wrote: **“NE#1, a police captain, engaged in social media activity that negatively impacted SPD’s ability to serve the public.”** It also wrote: **“NE#1 swore to protect and serve the entire community, including those with differing beliefs and characteristics.”** These are useful exact excerpts connecting the posts to official responsibilities rather than merely an offensive personal opinion. [3] Source: [OPA summary, p. 4](https://www.seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/OPA/ClosedCaseSummaries/2023OPA-0194ccs10-10-24.pdf).
- **Racial-bias evidence limitation:** OPA discussed posts referring to Black suspects as “thugs” and “animals,” but its footnote 4 expressly says it **could not independently locate** the quoted March 24 “violent animals” tweet, which it attributed to a May 3, 2023 PubliCola article. Do not describe every reproduced tweet as independently recovered by OPA. Its sustained findings and its limitation should both be preserved. [3] Source: [OPA summary, pp. 2 and 4](https://www.seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/OPA/ClosedCaseSummaries/2023OPA-0194ccs10-10-24.pdf).

**Editorial use:** This record supports describing an established history of anti-trans social-media conduct and sustained Seattle policy violations. It does not establish that Swank unlawfully discriminated against a particular person during an arrest, that he committed a crime, or that CJTC has revoked his certification.

## 3. CJTC: current database snapshot, not recycled historical counts

All **19 returned rows** identify first name Keith. Parsed the saved JSON, deduplicated by case identifier, and independently checked a server-side grouped count restricted to both last and first names. Both counts agree: **13 Investigative Queue, 2 Investigation, 4 Closed**. Thus there are **15 non-closed published cases**, but only **two marked active Investigation**. [4] Source: [live filtered official JSON](https://data.wa.gov/resource/r5ki-dmfz.json?%24where=upper%28officer_name%29%3D%27SWANK%27&%24limit=1000).
- **Meaning of status:** The agency defines Investigative Queue as awaiting an investigator; Investigation as active investigation; and Closed as concluded with a final outcome. Its introduction warns that the dataset can change during case-management updates. Do not equate every complaint or queued case with an active investigation. [5] Source: [official database and data dictionary](https://data.wa.gov/stories/s/WSCJTC-Certification-Database/3xkp-u89m/).

## 4. Charter Amendment No. 53: conditional January 1, 2027 change

- **Official ballot title:** *Charter Amendment No. 53 - Appointed Sheriff and Termination of Elected Sheriff*. The county's ballot-title/explanatory-statement PDF says the Charter Review Commission proposed amending **Section 3.70**. [6] Source: [official ballot text](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/159853/Charter-53---Explanatory-and-Ballot-Title).
- **Exact operative excerpt:** **“If approved, Sheriff shall cease to be an elective office, and the current elected term of office shall terminate early, effective January 1, 2027.”** It continues: **“The Sheriff will be appointed by the Executive and confirmed by a majority of the County Council.”** [6] Source: [official ballot text](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/159853/Charter-53---Explanatory-and-Ballot-Title).
- **Current versus proposed:** The explanatory statement identifies the current office as nonpartisan and elective. Appointment and early termination are conditional on approval. As of this dossier's November 2 Pacific cutoff, do not report the measure as passed or Swank as already removed. This is an institutional charter proposal, not a CJTC disciplinary ruling. [6] Source: [official explanatory statement](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/159853/Charter-53---Explanatory-and-Ballot-Title).

**Limit:** This session verified the official ballot title and explanatory statement, not a complete redline of Section 3.70 or any litigation docket. Do not infer a specific successor, hiring timetable, appointment eligibility, or lawsuit outcome from this one-page source.

## 5. November 1, 2026 Seattle Reign protest: organizing evidence and its limits

### Published email screenshot inspected

- **Original publisher located:** Defend Washington's Instagram carousel, co-posted with Fuse Washington, includes an embedded screenshot of an organizing email. The carousel was opened in a real browser and its email graphic inspected at full image resolution. This is direct inspection of the campaign-published screenshot, not independent authentication of the underlying email account or raw message. [8] Source: [Defend Washington/Fuse Washington carousel](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfITKYqlh2k/).
- **Visible subject and signature:** The email subject is **“Stand up for women and girls”** and the message is signed **“Keith.”** Its body says **“There are a total of 87 in our group.”** The campaign's large commentary above it instead says “at least 90.” Prefer the email's 87, attributed to Swank's claim, and never turn either number into a verified attendance figure. [8] Source: [carousel, third slide](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfITKYqlh2k/).
- **Visible event and ticket instructions:** The screenshot reads **“Seattle Reign v Orlando Pride”**, **“11/1/26 2:00 PM”**, and **“Buy your tickets in the \"Supporters Section.\" That's what it's called.”** The quotation is transcribed from the displayed email, including the period inside the section-name quotation marks. [8] Source: [email screenshot in carousel](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfITKYqlh2k/).
- **Exact conceal/reveal instruction:** **“Go into the stadium with your shirt covered, and at half time, put it on full display.”** This directly supports a planned concealed-entry/halftime shirt reveal. “Provoke the crowd” appears in the campaign's commentary, not in this visible email sentence, so attribute that characterization rather than putting it in Swank's mouth. [8] Source: [email screenshot in carousel](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfITKYqlh2k/).
- **Screenshot authentication limits:** The visible email date says only **“Yesterday.”** It does not independently establish the calendar sending date, delivery headers, complete recipient list, or the identity of every sender/recipient. No raw `.eml`, mailbox access, or message-header authentication was obtained. [8] Source inspected: [published screenshot](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfITKYqlh2k/).

### Independent reporting and aftermath

- **Contemporaneous organizing report:** Peter Talbot's October 30, 2026 News Tribune article says the relevant message was sent October 26, attributes the 87-person group claim to Swank, and quotes the same shirt instruction. It also quotes his later message: **“I posted that we should do this, and I organized it, just like I said I would.”** The report says Swank and Reign FC had not responded to requests for comment. Attribute these additional email details to the newspaper because that later message was not independently authenticated in this session. [7] Source: [Talbot, The News Tribune](https://www.thenewstribune.com/news/local/article317852433.html).
- **Reported recruitment:** The News Tribune describes October 24 social posts seeking people to wear XX-XY Athletics shirts and attend a sporting event, and reports Swank's support for Initiative 638. The source supports organizing and public promotion, not his physical presence at the match. [7] Source: [October 30 report](https://www.thenewstribune.com/news/local/article317852433.html).
- **Aftermath is observational, not a head count:** Mathew Rodriguez's November 2 Them report reproduces eyewitness Katrina Lehto's November 1 post: **“There might be... six of them?”** It includes photos described as a surrounding “flag fortress.” That wording is explicitly tentative. Do not publish “six protesters attended,” “only six of his 87 supporters came,” or that Swank himself attended. No verified total or organizer-attendance evidence was established here. [9] Source: [Them report and embedded eyewitness posts](https://www.them.us/news/seattle-reign-match-trans-protest).

## 6. Publication guardrails

- Keep the three evidence levels separate: completed OPA findings, unresolved/administratively closed CJTC cases, and campaign-published protest emails supported by contemporary reporting.
- The strongest accountability connection is the documented contrast between a sheriff organizing against trans inclusion and OPA's earlier sustained findings about anti-trans posts undermining trust. It is not necessary to exaggerate attendance, claim he was fired, or portray allegations as convictions.
- Do not attribute every stadium participant to Swank's list, infer a confirmed attendance count from photographs, or present his organizing as proof that he personally attended.
- Treat Defend Washington as an interested campaign source. Its published email image is inspectable evidence, but its commentary is not an official finding or a substitute for the initiative's primary text.
- This dossier does not independently verify every current NWSL player's gender identity or medical history, and does not adopt the campaign's universal roster claim as a separately established fact.
- A full statutory assessment of Initiative 638, NWSL policy, or protest participants' removal would require its own primary-source checks. No such removal is established by this dossier.
