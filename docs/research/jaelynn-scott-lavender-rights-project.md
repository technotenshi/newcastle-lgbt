- **Mini versus full reporting:** Scott's page says she opted for full reporting. McCorkle's says mini reporting. The PDC's rules say mini reporting is for campaigns that will raise and spend no more than $7,000 and that these campaigns do not file contribution and expenditure reports. So McCorkle's $750 and $23.12 are amounts shown on the PDC page, not necessarily complete current totals, and they are not directly comparable to Scott's full-reporting totals. This does not show unreported activity. It is a disclosure limit. Do not reuse the figures as complete totals in later articles. Source: https://www.pdc.wa.gov/registration-reporting/candidates-committees/registration-reporting-basics/choosing-mini-or-full-reporting
- Scott's contributions by PDC category: individual $107,932.45, other $24,212.50, political action committee $7,500.00, business $7,385.15, miscellaneous $8,549.22.
- The ChatGPT reports gave $154,352 raised and $108,041 spent (via a secondary site, Washington Independents). The PDC pages show higher figures, so use the PDC and date every figure.
- The PDC page for Scott lists Chipalo Street among "candidates filed for this position" with $20,000 raised. That looks like leftover filing data for his Senate move, not a 2026 contest. Do not report it as a third candidate.

## 2. Jaelynn Scott

- Executive director of Lavender Rights Project. First run for office (KNKX).
- Grew up in Mississippi, is an ordained Buddhist minister who no longer practices, moved to the Central District in late 2024, and has worked in the district for nearly a decade (Standard).
- Master of Divinity in Boulder, Colorado (The Urbanist). Naropa University is named by Erin in the Morning and Wikipedia.
- Runs as a Democratic Socialist (KNKX). The Standard says she could be one of only two Democratic Socialists of America members in the Legislature, with Rep. Shaun Scott of the 43rd District (not related).
- **Positions (KNKX, October 24):** "raise more progressive revenue sooner," a tax on households earning more than $1 million a year, no expansion to lower incomes, relief on sales and property taxes, state backfill of federal Medicaid cuts. She says the two anti-trans initiatives aim to make scapegoats of trans people, and said "We will not be distracted."
- **Standard (August 13):** wants revenue spent on housing construction, education, and health care, wants more publicly owned housing, supports legislation to bar Washington law enforcement from employing former federal immigration agents hired under President Trump, and spoke at the rally launching the "No Hate in WA State" campaign.
- **Endorsements named by Erin in the Morning:** Sen. Bernie Sanders, Rep. Pramila Jayapal, the state Democratic Socialists of America and Working Families Party branches, and district and county Democrats. The Seattle Times printed no endorsement in this race (headline of its October 16 editorial, body paywalled).
- Photo: Wikimedia Commons "Jaelynn Scott (Seattle).jpg," taken February 15, 2026, by JPS10000, released under CC0.

**Open items:**
- **Year she joined Lavender Rights Project:** The Urbanist says 2019. Wikipedia, citing a 2024 Seattle Times profile (paywalled), says she moved to Seattle in 2017, was later hired, and became executive director in 2020. Do not print a year until reconciled.
- **The campaign's "$16 million" figure** for jail-divestment money redirected to housing. It was not found in a primary record. The ChatGPT reports describe component amounts of about $6.6 million and $4.4 million from a county budget packet, which this check did not re-read. Do not repeat the $16 million without a county record.

- **Form 990, fiscal year ending December 2024 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer):** revenue $3,967,987, expenses $3,156,302, net assets $4,989,414. Scott's reported compensation is $179,045, with $15,892 in other compensation. Fiscal year 2023: revenue $2,782,255, expenses $2,319,431.

The Standard credits LRP with helping lead the push to open it.

## 4. History claims: handle "first" with care

The article dated October 28 claims only Washington's "first openly transgender legislator," attributed to the Standard and KNKX. The national claims below were checked and left out on purpose.

| Person | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Althea Garrison (MA) | Elected 1992 as a Republican, Black. Outed against her wishes after the election and came out publicly in an October 2023 interview with The 19th. | Rutgers CAWP |
| Danica Roem (VA) | Elected 2017. Victory Fund called her the first out trans person to win and serve in a state legislature. | Victory Fund |
| Stephanie Byers (KS) | Elected 2020. Victory Fund called her the first out trans person of color elected to a state legislature. | Victory Fund |
| Mauree "Mo" Turner (OK) | Elected 2020. Black, nonbinary, Muslim. The 19th calls them the first out nonbinary state legislator in U.S. history and the first out transgender lawmaker in Oklahoma's statehouse. Erin in the Morning calls them the first out Black trans person in a state legislature. | The 19th, Erin in the Morning |
| Andrea Jenkins (MN) | Elected to the Minneapolis City Council in 2017. First African American openly transgender woman elected to public office in the United States. | National Women's History Museum |

- **The Standard says** Scott "will also be the first Black openly trans state lawmaker elected in the country." Erin in the Morning's account of Turner conflicts with that line. A defensible narrower wording is "first openly trans Black woman elected to a U.S. state legislature," attributed to its source, but it was not published.
- **Wikipedia's lead says** Scott would be "the first openly transgender Black woman to hold political office in the United States." That conflicts with Jenkins and should not be used.
- **Other 2026 Washington candidates:** SGN profiled Hannah Sabio Howell (43rd District Senate) and Erin Chapman Smith (29th District House position 2) alongside Scott as queer and transgender candidates. Chapman-Smith lost the August 4 primary (Ballotpedia). Howell's identity and status were not confirmed. No other openly trans candidate for the Legislature on the November ballot has been confirmed.
