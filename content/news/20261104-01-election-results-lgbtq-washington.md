### I-638: projected to pass, banning trans girls from girls' sports

I-638 would bar transgender girls from girls' interschool sports: its text closes the covered female competitions to students it calls "biologically male."[^9] The attorney general's explanation says it would generally prevent transgender girls from joining girls' teams at public K–12 schools and at private schools participating in WIAA or similar organizations.[^10] To enter those competitions, a student must provide a statement from their personal health care provider verifying what the text calls their "biological sex," **based on reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup, or normal endogenous testosterone levels.**[^9]

**This measure harms girls' sports, including both cisgender and transgender girls.** It makes medical sex verification a condition of entering the covered girls' competitions, permits reproductive anatomy as a basis for that verification, and imposes **no corresponding requirement on boys' competitions.**[^9][^10] Girls' participation would depend on medical scrutiny of their bodies, while transgender girls face exclusion from their teams. Family physician **Dr. Madie Gore**, in a statement opposing the measure, warned of "unnecessary genital exams" and stress and harm for girls.[^11] This is a discriminatory barrier to participation and bodily privacy, not a protection for girls' sports.

Let's Go Washington told voters that I-638 bans no one and that a doctor could use "any other method" to check a box on an existing form.[^8] Neither claim survives the text. It excludes trans girls from girls' teams, and it requires the provider to verify sex "relying only on" anatomy, genetics or testosterone levels.[^9] Voters are projected to have approved a measure its own campaign misdescribed.

In King County, home to Newcastle, voters are rejecting it: **No** has 336,255 votes, or 57.25%, against 251,111 **Yes** votes, or 42.75%.[^2]

The solidarity Reign supporters showed when they answered Sheriff Keith Swank's anti-trans protest with trans pride flags, described in [our earlier report](/news/2026/11/02/sheriff-anti-trans-protest-reign-match), matters more now, not less. A statewide vote does not change who belongs on the team. Trans girls are girls, and this site will keep opposing I-638 and standing with the students it targets.

### I-001: voters are rejecting the rollback of student privacy

I-001 would undo every 2025 amendment to the parents' rights statute and restore the version enacted through Initiative 2081.[^12] The attorney general's explanation lists what students would lose. The exception for a staff member's private notes would be eliminated and records would be due within 10 business days. The statute's right to immediate notice when a child is allegedly abused by a school employee or contractor would be removed, and so would the right to file a harassment, intimidation or bullying complaint on a child's behalf.[^12]

The initiative's strikeout text deletes the statute's express guarantee of an education free from discrimination on grounds including sexual orientation, gender expression and gender identity.[^13] In its place, the attorney general says, parents would receive an assurance that schools will not discriminate based on the family's "sincerely held religious beliefs."[^12] Separate prohibitions elsewhere in state law remain, but this statute would stop naming LGBTQ+ students as protected while naming religious belief. Paired with wider, faster parental access to school-held records, including "records of any mental health counseling," that is a direct risk to a student who confides in a counselor before they are safe to come out at home.[^13]

If the result holds, voters will have stopped this attack on student privacy. On November 5, the **No Hate in WA State** campaign said Washington "made history" by rejecting I-001; the November 4 count showed No ahead with 52.62%.[^14][^5] I-001 was itself an attempt to reinstate a 2024 initiative after the Legislature amended it: a defeat at the ballot is one round, not the end of the campaign against LGBTQ+ students' privacy.

### I-645: voters are rejecting the repeal of the tax on income over $1 million

I-645 would repeal the 9.9% tax on annual individual income over $1 million and prohibit specified state and local income taxes. The tax applies beginning with **2028 income**; married couples and registered domestic partners share a single **$1 million standard deduction**. Repeal would eliminate future revenue for public education, health care and other human services, as well as child care and early learning.[^15] A measure written to protect income above $1 million would take that money from the public services LGBTQ+ people and their families rely on.

Let's Go Washington warned that lawmakers could lower the threshold to reach everyone.[^8] That is a prediction about future legislatures, not what the law says.[^15] Heywood told the Washington State Standard he expected the campaign to cost his committee more than $10 million.[^7] Our [earlier election article](/news/2026/10/28/washington-election-transgender-rights) urged a No vote. Voters are rejecting it by a wider margin than I-001, 57.93% No; if that holds, voters will have refused to trade public services for a tax break at the very top.

## I-638: the threat to students and the work ahead

For trans girls, the stakes are a place on the team alongside their peers. A vote cannot make those children less deserving of friendship, belonging or the chance to play.

### What advocates are saying

Before the election, **ACLU of Washington** warned that the damage goes beyond eligibility. In an October 2 commentary, its media and communications strategist **LaVendrick Smith** wrote that sports bans on transgender girls "send the hateful message that some children don't belong simply because of who they are."[^16]

The **No Hate in WA State** coalition responded to the first returns on November 3. **Adrianna Suluai**, UTOPIA Washington's policy and advocacy director and a coalition executive-board member, promised continued support regardless of the outcome:[^17]

> "To all the trans kids, to all of the girls playing elementary, middle and high school sports, we care about you and there are people who will never stop fighting for you."
> *Adrianna Suluai, in No Hate in WA State's November 3 response*[^17]

After the projection, campaign manager **Libby Watson** called the I-638 result "a disappointment" and said: "The fight is not over, it is just beginning." Her November 5 statement makes the same case this article does: while trans girls are the most affected, the measure "has the potential to harm any girl who wishes to play sports through its invasive exam requirements." She spoke directly to the students it targets:[^14]

> "To all trans girls participating in school sports in Washington, we want you to know that you belong: on the field, on the court, on the team. We stand with you and will continue to fight for a day when you can play sports without controversy."
> *Libby Watson, No Hate in WA State campaign manager, November 5*[^14]

### What comes next: standing with trans students

If the result holds, I-638 takes effect **December 3, 2026**, according to the state's fiscal analysis, and applies going forward rather than retroactively. The analysis expects the Office of the Superintendent of Public Instruction (OSPI) to update its guidance and school districts to revise their policies and procedures.[^18] The next few weeks matter: those policies are about to be rewritten, and parents, coaches, students and neighbors can show up at school board meetings to insist that their district protects students' medical privacy and keeps every child connected to their team and school.

Washington's constitution also shapes what the Legislature can do. For two years after voters approve a measure, lawmakers can amend it only with a two-thirds vote of all members elected to each chamber; after that, ordinary legislation can amend or repeal it, and voters can amend or repeal it at any election.[^19] Two-thirds means 66 House members and 33 senators. The House scenario in the Olympia section below clears that line; the Senate scenario falls two seats short. Fixing I-638 before December 2028 will take more votes than this election delivered, so every Senate seat and every legislator's position on it matters.

As of November 4, we found no filed lawsuit against I-638, no injunction and no post-election guidance from OSPI or WIAA. On November 5, No Hate in WA State said many consider I-638 "likely unconstitutional" and that legal groups and organizations are considering litigation.[^14] We will report any challenge when it is filed.

Families facing exclusion or harassment can contact their school's civil-rights coordinator, use OSPI's discrimination-complaint process or reach the **Office of the Education Ombuds**, all listed on the Washington State LGBTQ Commission's sports resource page.[^20] No Hate in WA State points anyone who needs support, and anyone who wants to help, to the trans-led organizations active in the campaign: [UTOPIA Washington](https://utopiawa.org/), [TRACTION](https://tractionpnw.org/), [Lavender Rights Project](https://www.lavenderrightsproject.org/) (led by Jaelynn Scott), [Oasis Youth Center](https://www.oasisyouthcenter.org/), [Ingersoll Gender Center](https://www.ingersollgendercenter.org/), [Gender Justice League](https://genderjusticeleague.org/) and [Diversity Alliance of Puget Sound](https://www.diversityallianceofthepugetsound.org/). Volunteer with them or donate.[^14] Supporting trans students means showing up on the sidelines, at board meetings and in everyday school life, so that no child is pushed out of their community by a vote. Neither the unfinished count nor the projected passage is a reason to leave trans children to face this alone.

## Jaelynn Scott: a trans woman in the Legislature as trans students are targeted

The official November count gives **Jaelynn Scott** 32,069 votes, or 87.32%, against **Evon McCorkle's** 4,344, or 11.83%, with 311 write-ins. Her lead is 27,725 votes.[^2] LGBTQ Nation reports that she has won and describes her as Washington's first trans legislator and the first out Black trans woman elected to serve in a U.S. state legislature.[^3]

The timing matters. In the same election that is projected to ban trans girls from girls' sports, 37th District voters gave a trans woman 87% of the vote. As the Legislature, school districts and OSPI respond to I-638, trans Washingtonians will have one of their own in the room, not just allies speaking about them.

Scott leads the **Lavender Rights Project**, a Black trans feminist advocacy organization, and is a member of the Democratic Socialists of America.[^3] [Our earlier profile](/news/2026/10/28/jaelynn-scott-first-openly-trans-legislator) explains the work and priorities behind the candidacy. Representation matters because a person arrives with experience, commitments and a constituency, not only a milestone.

Stating her milestone precisely honors the trans people who came before her. The words **openly**, **woman** and **state legislature** matter. Rutgers records that Althea Garrison, a Black trans woman, was elected to the Massachusetts House in 1992 and was outed against her wishes after the election.[^21] Mo Turner, who is Black and nonbinary, previously served in Oklahoma's legislature.[^22] Andrea Jenkins became the first openly transgender African American woman elected to U.S. public office in 2017, at the Minneapolis City Council level.[^23] Scott's narrower distinction must not erase those predecessors.

Her LD37 race was not on Newcastle ballots. The local races covered in our guide were LD41 and WA-9; their current returns follow.

**Sheriff Keith Swank** organized the anti-trans demonstration at the November 1 Reign match. *The News Tribune* reported emails in which he told a group of 87 people to buy tickets and reveal XX-XY Athletics shirts at halftime, and quoted him: "I posted that we should do this, and I organized it, just like I said I would."[^35] Over social-media posts disparaging transgender people that he made as a Seattle police captain, a Seattle Police Department disciplinary report sustained findings against him for bias-free policing, professionalism and social-media violations, and concluded: "If you were still a Department employee, I would have terminated your employment."[^36] [Our Reign report](/news/2026/11/02/sheriff-anti-trans-protest-reign-match) has the full record.

**Amendment 53** would make the sheriff appointed by the county executive and confirmed by a Council majority, ending Swank's elected term on January 1, 2027.[^37] It is the one measure on the ballot that would have removed him early, and it is losing by roughly two to one. If rejection holds, Swank serves out his term.

**Amendment 54** would create a Public Safety Ombuds department for independent oversight of the sheriff and his employees, subject to legal limits. It would publish findings and recommendations, refer complaints to appropriate authorities and establish a community advisory committee.[^38] Voters are rejecting it too, 52.31% to 47.69%.[^6] If rejection holds, Pierce County keeps a sheriff with a documented anti-trans record and no new independent office to watch his department.

Trans residents of Pierce County, and the trans fans and players who travel to Lumen Field, deserve policing that treats them as people to protect, not targets to organize against. If these results hold, that work continues through complaints, public records and the next sheriff election.

Control of Congress is a means, not the goal. ## Representation milestones beyond Washington

This is a selected national overview, not an exhaustive list. These candidates won, according to CNN, with AP reporting confirming the distinctions for Pappas, Flanagan, Bottoms, Haaland and Donalds.[^45][^46]

**Juliana Stratton's** Illinois Senate victory will pair her with **Tammy Duckworth**, making Illinois the first state represented by two women of color in the Senate. Duckworth is of Thai heritage.[^46] That is a milestone in the state's delegation, not a new 2026 election victory for Duckworth.

Historic representation deserves recognition, and it does not erase a record. As a U.S. representative, **Byron Donalds** voted against the Respect for Marriage Act in 2022 and for the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act in 2023, a federal bill to bar transgender girls and women from girls' and women's school sports.[^48][^49] Florida's first Black governor has voted against LGBTQ+ people, and we will hold him to that record.
