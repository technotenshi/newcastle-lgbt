:Dateline Washington voters passed Initiative 638 on Tuesday with about 57% of the vote. It bars transgender girls from girls' school sports, and it does something its backers rarely mention: it makes every girl who wants to join a girls' school team get a health care provider to verify her sex first.[^1][^2] The count continues, and the measure is set to take effect on **December 3**.[^1] This site [opposed I-638](/news/2026/10/28/washington-election-transgender-rights) and still does. Below is the harm to every girl, who is responsible, and what comes next.

## Every girl on a school team will need a medical sign-off

I-638 requires a student who elects to compete in girls' school activities to submit a statement from her personal health care provider verifying her "biological sex," relying only on reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup, or normal endogenously produced testosterone levels. Boys' competitions have no such requirement.[^3] The attorney general's explanation says the students affected "would thus have to undergo some type of medical evaluation of their sex."[^4]

Read what that means for a girl whose provider has no record that settles the question. The anatomy route is a genital inspection. KUOW reported that a provider can do it without touching the child, and that it would likely be the most accessible option for most kids. The alternatives are a genetic test or a hormone test.[^5] A Spokane family physician, Dr. Libby Loft, put the uninsured prices of those two tests at $1,310 and $162, and says the text leaves a provider unable to rely on a birth certificate.[^6]

The text does not specify a method. It names the routine sports physical as the vehicle and limits the provider to those three bases, and FactHub notes that it does not rule out a provider relying on what they already know from a girl's past care.[^3][^7] That gap is the harm. WIAA says its implementation details, "including updates to requirements with physicals," are still under legal review, less than a month before the law takes effect.[^1] Every family is waiting to learn how much of their daughter's body a form will demand.

The girls who lose most are predictable:

- Girls without a regular doctor or insurance, who face the cost and the exam.
- Survivors of sexual assault, who could be retraumatized, according to No Hate in WA State campaign manager Libby Watson.[^8]
- Girls whose families decline to submit them to an inspection and so leave the team.

Soccer star Megan Rapinoe, who spent most of her career with the Seattle Reign, called this "a feature, not a bug" of such laws, saying backers "will drive thousands of girls out of sports because their parents cannot afford the necessary testing or refuse to submit their daughters to genital inspections."[^8] The 19th reports that none of the 27 states with similar laws require student-athletes to verify their birth sex, and that Florida dropped testing language from its own law after criticism that it could be abused.[^8]

More than 140 health care providers signed a statement opposing the measure. Pediatric nurse Kelsey Gellner, a Washington State Nurses Association board member, said: "I can say with confidence that girls do not want to have an unnecessary exam that creates an unsafe barrier."[^9] The ACLU of Washington put it plainly: the law subjects all girls, and only girls, to "invasive sex verification procedures."[^2]

## The number behind the ban is small

State officials estimated five to 10 transgender athletes among more than 200,000 student-athletes statewide, though no official data exists.[^6] WIAA says it is aware of about 10 transgender middle and high school athletes.[^8] KUOW reports there may be more, since WIAA does not require schools to disclose that information.[^5] That is an estimate, not a census: WIAA says it keeps no definitive count because "transgender students are not identified by state law."[^10]

Let's Go Washington's answer to that number is that it does not matter. Spokesperson **Hallie Herzberg** said one girl losing a podium spot "is enough for us to take action."[^6] The cost of that action lands on every girl who joins a team, and on the trans girls among those athletes, who lose their place on girls' teams. The existence of other teams does not make that exclusion disappear.

## Who is responsible

**Brian Heywood**, the hedge fund manager who founded Let's Go Washington, the political committee behind I-638, sponsored it.[^8] On election night he said: "Five years from now, I hope girl athletes don't know this fight took place, because that will mean that boys know their place is not in girls' sports."[^11] Trans girls are girls. That sentence calls them boys and tells them where they belong.

Heywood also told KUOW that implementation is simple: "a doctor goes in, they've never met you before. They go and they open up the MyChart. They go, 'Oh, she's a girl.' They can check a box that says she's a girl."[^1] That does not work for a girl whose doctor has no record of her anatomy, which is exactly the girl without a regular doctor. It is also not in the text of the ban, as KUOW noted.[^1] Family physician Dr. Grace Shih told KUOW earlier this year that she does a separate exam for sports physicals, and she would not feel comfortable using a checkup to satisfy one.[^1]

Herzberg said the initiative merely restores a checkbox removed from the sports physical form in 2019, and called concerns "not the reality of the initiative at all."[^6][^8] The initiative's own findings claim that existing physicals already include "documentation of the student's sex assigned at birth."[^3] But The 19th reports that the state requirement was rolled back in 2019, that current state-recommended forms do not ask for verification of sex, and that sports physicals do not often include a genital exam, according to the National Federation of State High School Associations.[^8] The measure adds something new. Let's Go Washington disputes that it would require invasive or expensive exams, and the text does not settle that dispute.[^6] That is the problem with writing a mandate and leaving the method to someone else.

**WIAA's member schools** voted 29-24 in April for an advisory proposal to bar transgender girls from girls' sports. WIAA said it would not implement the proposal because it conflicts with state law.[^6] Voters have now removed that obstacle.

**The state Democratic Party** wavered within a day. Party chair **Shasti Conrad** told KUOW on Wednesday she planned to fight the ban in court, then the party told KUOW on Thursday it did not plan to litigate.[^1] Conrad called the result "incredibly disheartening," and the party pledged support to groups seeking to overturn the ban.[^11] That support is welcome. Reversing within a day on whether the party itself would litigate is not what trans kids needed from the state's largest party.

## What happens next

**Court.** Gender Justice League, a Seattle LGBTQ advocacy group, told KUOW it may challenge the ban on privacy grounds. Executive director **Danni Askini** said: "If there is an avenue, we will challenge this ballot measure in court on behalf of our members and our community and trans youth."[^1] The theory rests on a 2008 Washington Supreme Court ruling, *York v. Wahkiakum School District*, in which the court held that a school district's random, suspicionless drug testing of student athletes violated the state constitution's privacy protection. Four justices wrote the lead opinion and rejected a "special needs" exception outright; four others concurred in the result on narrower grounds, and one more concurred separately, so how far the ruling reaches is a question for lawyers.[^12] The state constitution matters because the federal route is closed: on June 30 the U.S. Supreme Court held in *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* that states "may maintain women's and girls' sports for biological females," in the Court's words, consistent with Title IX and the Equal Protection Clause, in an opinion six justices joined. The ruling does not require states to pass such laws.[^13] The ACLU of Washington said it will "explore all potential next steps" but told KUOW, "As an organization, we don't speak about potential litigation."[^1][^2] No Hate in WA State says many consider I-638 "likely unconstitutional" and that legal groups and organizations are considering litigation.[^14] As of November 6, we found no announced lawsuit. KUOW notes WIAA's spokesperson declined further comment "due to pending litigation" without naming a case, and we cannot say whether that refers to I-638.[^1]

**Rules.** WIAA, school district boards and the Office of the Superintendent of Public Instruction (OSPI) will write the rules that decide how invasive verification is. The text puts the policies on a school district board or a "voluntary nonprofit entity," and state law lets a board hand control of interschool athletics to WIAA.[^3] The state's fiscal analysis expects OSPI to update its guidance and districts to revise their policies and procedures.[^15] WIAA says it is preparing "necessary changes" by December 3 and will give schools updated forms.[^1] The form WIAA drafts is the one that reaches every family.

**The Legislature.** For two years after voters approve a measure, lawmakers can amend it only with a two-thirds vote of each chamber, 66 House members and 33 senators.[^16] On current leads, the House clears that bar, with 67 Democratic leaders, and the Senate falls two seats short, at 31 Democrats, so every senator's position matters. See our [election results report](/news/2026/11/04/election-results-lgbtq-washington) for the seat-by-seat count. After two years, ordinary legislation can change it.

## What you can do

- **Go to school board meetings.** Districts are about to write the procedures. Ask your board to adopt the least invasive reading of the law, to accept existing medical records, to protect students' medical privacy, and to keep every girl on her team.
- **Press WIAA.** A board may hand this job to WIAA, and WIAA's forms will reach every family. Ask it to accept records a girl's provider already holds and to require nothing more invasive than the text demands.
- **Tell your legislators** that I-638 must be amended as soon as the law allows, and ask where they stand, especially if you live in a Senate district that decides the two-thirds math.
- **Donate or volunteer** with trans-led groups that were active in the campaign. Watson's statement lists UTOPIA Washington, TRACTIONPNW, Lavender Rights Project, Oasis Youth Center, Ingersoll Gender Center, Gender Justice League and Diversity Alliance of Puget Sound.[^14]
- **Show up for the girls it targets.** Seattle Reign fans answered an anti-trans demonstration with trans pride flags, as [we reported](/news/2026/11/02/sheriff-anti-trans-protest-reign-match). A statewide vote does not change who belongs on the team.

I-638 adds a medical barrier for every girl on a school team and removes trans girls from their teams. Newcastle's LGBTQ+ neighbors will keep opposing it at school board meetings, in Olympia and at the ballot, and we will report each step.
