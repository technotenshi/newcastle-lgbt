:Dateline Seattle Reign supporters answered an anti-trans demonstration at Lumen Field on Sunday, November 1, with a display of transgender Pride flags, according to photographs and eyewitness posts reported by *Them*.[^1] The demonstration followed organizing by **Pierce County Sheriff Keith Swank**, who urged a group to buy tickets and reveal XX-XY Athletics shirts at halftime, according to emails reviewed by *The News Tribune*.[^2] The Reign, meanwhile, beat the Orlando Pride 1-0 and clinched a playoff berth in front of 16,095 fans.[^3]

The supporters' response deserves attention on its own terms. They made support for trans people visible at a women's sporting event that Swank had chosen for an anti-trans display. That is a different understanding of supporting women's sports: showing up for the team without treating transgender neighbors as people who do not belong.

## What happened in the stands

On Bluesky, spectator **Katrina Lehto** posted a photograph of the supporters' section with trans Pride flags gathered around a small area of seating. Her November 1 post said: "There might be... six of them? They're brigaded by trans flags."[^4]

That is an eyewitness estimate, not a verified count. The photograph shows numerous light blue, pink, and white flags, including flags bearing the words "Trans Rights Are Human Rights." Neither the photograph nor the reporting establishes the total number of anti-trans demonstrators or whether Swank himself attended.[^1][^4]

The match was also an important occasion for the players and their supporters. The club had announced it as captain **Jess Fishlock's** final regular-season home appearance, at the end of a 14-year career in Seattle.[^5] Defender **Jordyn Bugg** scored the winning goal, and the victory secured Seattle's place in the postseason.[^3] Those achievements deserve the attention that a sheriff's political messaging sought to claim.

## Swank's instructions were explicit

*The News Tribune* reported on October 30 that **Defend Washington**, a political action committee opposing the school-sports initiative I-638, had supplied emails about the planned demonstration. According to the newspaper, Swank wrote in an October 26 email that there were **87 people in the group** and directed them to purchase tickets for the November 1 match.[^2] That number describes the organizing group, not the number who bought tickets or attended.

The instructions included concealment followed by a coordinated reveal:

> "Go into the stadium with your shirt covered, and at half time, put it on full display."[^2]
> *Keith Swank, October 26 email, quoted by The News Tribune*

The newspaper reported that the targeted seating was in the area where the **Royal Guard**, the Reign's supporters' group, often waves Pride flags. After the reporter tried to contact Swank, the sheriff sent another email complaining of an "infiltrator" who had told the newspaper about the event.[^2]

His response also acknowledged his role: "I posted that we should do this, and I organized it, just like I said I would."[^2]

Swank's public framing was that participants would "stand up for free speech," the newspaper reported.[^2] But the coordinated halftime display was also a political intervention in a women's match, in a section known for LGBTQ+ visibility. Supporting free expression does not oblige other supporters to agree with that message or leave it unanswered. Their own flags were an answer.

## The shirts carry a political message

**XX-XY Athletics** is not simply an unrelated sportswear label caught in a controversy. Its own mission page says it is "unfair and dangerous" to allow people it calls "males (XY)" to compete in girls' and women's sports, and says women's sports must "remain female." The page also says the company donates to organizations including **ICONS Women** and its own athletics fund.[^6] Those are the company's claims and political commitments, not a neutral medical description of transgender women.

That context explains why Swank selected the brand. The newspaper reported that he promoted the demonstration while supporting I-638, and that an October 25 post denied there was "such thing" as a transgender boy or girl.[^2] This was not merely a disagreement about the details of one competition's eligibility rules. Denying transgender identities makes the target broader than a rulebook.

## A record of anti-trans rhetoric

Swank's organizing follows a documented record. A **Seattle Police Department** disciplinary report dated January 30, 2025, names Swank and records sustained findings for bias-free policing, professionalism, and social-media policy violations in case **OPA 23-0194**. The report describes posts disparaging transgender people and concludes: "If you were still a Department employee, I would have terminated your employment."[^7]

Swank had already retired, and the chief explicitly stated that no discipline was being imposed.[^7] The findings nevertheless bear directly on the trust residents should be able to place in a law-enforcement leader. Fair treatment must not depend on a resident's gender identity.

## What I-638 would actually do

Washington voters will decide I-638 on **Tuesday, November 3**. The measure is backed by **Let's Go Washington**, the political action committee founded by hedge fund executive and Republican donor **Brian Heywood**.[^8] Our [October 28 analysis](/news/2026/10/28/washington-election-transgender-rights) explains the campaign's wider agenda and its implications for transgender students.

The proposal concerns school sports, not professional soccer. Its operative text would bar students it calls "biologically male" from specified girls' competitions. It would also require a student seeking to enter those girls' competitions to supply a form signed by their personal health care provider verifying their "biological sex," relying on reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup, or normal endogenously produced testosterone levels.[^9]

The attorney general's explanatory statement says the measure would generally prevent transgender girls from participating on girls' teams. It would affect public K–12 schools and private schools participating in the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association or similar organizations. The statement adds that students seeking to play in the covered girls' sports "would thus have to undergo some type of medical evaluation of their sex."[^10]

The text does **not** prescribe a genital examination for every girl. It allows more than one basis for verification, and the exact implementation is contested. But the medical verification requirement is real, and it is imposed on participation in the specified girls' competitions, not mirrored by a corresponding requirement for boys' competitions.[^9]

The campaign has also invoked pediatric medical authority. *The Seattle Times* reported that Let's Go Washington declined a request to remove mention of the **American Academy of Pediatrics** from its argument in the **state voters pamphlet**. Campaign spokesperson **Hallie Herzberg** defended the wording, while physician **Eloi Hoopman** explained that recommended annual wellness visits are different from sports physicals.[^11] An organization's recommendation for routine health care is not an endorsement of using medical sex verification to decide who may play.

## A separate vote on the sheriff's office

Pierce County voters also face **Charter Amendment 53**, which would change the sheriff from an elected position to one appointed by the county executive and confirmed by the County Council. If approved, it would take effect January 1, 2027, ending Swank's current term early.[^12]

This is a separate county governance decision, not a measure on Newcastle voters' ballots. The election has not yet concluded.

## Belonging should not end at the touchline

The supporters' flags did not settle the election. They did offer a visible refusal to let an anti-trans message speak for everyone who supports women's soccer.

Newcastle residents have a decision of their own to make on I-638. We recommend **voting no**: transgender girls should not be excluded from girls' teams, and access to those competitions should not depend on the initiative's medical sex-certification requirement. Protecting opportunities in sport should mean widening participation and supporting athletes, not making a targeted group the subject of a political spectacle.

**Vote tomorrow, November 3.** The secretary of state recommends using an official ballot drop box this close to Election Day; ballots must be in a drop box by **8 p.m.** Mailed ballots must be postmarked by Election Day, and late mailing risks a late postmark.[^13] Our [Newcastle voter's guide](/news/2026/10/28/newcastle-voters-guide) covers the local ballot recommendations.
