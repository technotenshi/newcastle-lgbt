:Dateline On November 3, Washington voters will decide two statewide initiatives that would bar transgender girls from girls' school sports and widen parents' access to student records, plus a third that would repeal the state's new tax on income over $1 million.[^1] All three are sponsored by Let's Go Washington, a political committee founded by hedge fund manager **Brian Heywood**.[^2][^3][^4] For transgender students and their families, this is not an abstract campaign issue. They are our neighbors, classmates, teammates, and friends.

Our recommendation is **vote no on I-638, no on I-001, and no on I-645**. The first would exclude trans girls from certain school competitions and require medical sex verification of the girls who play. The second would widen access to students' school records and strip a nondiscrimination clause from the parents' rights statute. The third would eliminate a source of funding for public services while protecting very high incomes from a tax scheduled to begin in 2028.[^5][^6][^7]

## I-638: Exclusion is not fairness

I-638 concerns interschool athletics at public K–12 schools and at private schools that participate in the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association (WIAA) or similar nonprofit organizations. Washington's attorney general says it would generally prevent transgender girls from participating on girls' sports teams.[^5]

The initiative's text goes further than exclusion. A girl who elects to compete in an event intended for female students must provide, as part of the routine sports physical, a form signed by her personal health care provider that verifies her "biological sex," "relying only on" one or more of three things: her reproductive anatomy, her genetic makeup, or her normal endogenously produced testosterone levels.[^8] The attorney general's office puts the effect plainly: students "would thus have to undergo some type of medical evaluation of their sex."[^5] The text **creates no matching requirement for a boy who wants to play on a boys' team,** and The Guardian confirms that boys are not required to undergo the same process.[^8][^9]

**That is a double standard,** not protection for girls. It makes access to girls' sports conditional on medical certification while boys pursue the corresponding opportunities without that hurdle. The requirement reaches cisgender as well as transgender girls, raising privacy concerns and potentially adding cost and delay for families. Kelsey Gellner, a pediatric nurse and Washington State Nurses Association board member, told The Guardian that girls "do not want to have an unnecessary exam that creates an unsafe barrier."[^9] Spokane family physician Libby Loft said a verification requirement adds another barrier at an age when girls are already twice as likely as boys to drop out of sports by 14.[^9] Opponents add that free sports physicals are often held in group settings, which could deepen the embarrassment for girls.[^10]

The group the measure singles out is very small. The WIAA has said it is aware of about 10 student athletes who identify as transgender out of more than 200,000 in the state.[^9] A rule that subjects every girl who wants to play to certification, in order to exclude about 10 students, is out of proportion to the problem it claims to solve.

Let's Go Washington's claims do not all hold up against the text:

- **"No bans, just sex-based rules."** The campaign says I-638 bans no one.[^2] The measure bars "biologically male students" from girls' competitions, which the attorney general says would generally prevent transgender girls from playing on girls' teams. The existence of other teams does not make that exclusion disappear.[^8][^5]
- **Doctors can use "any other method."** The campaign's FAQ says a doctor can use any method to check a box on an existing form.[^2] The measure's findings say the existing sports physical already documents sex assigned at birth, but its operative section requires a provider to verify sex "relying only on" anatomy, genetics, or testosterone levels.[^8] Loft said the wording means she "cannot rely on a birth certificate."[^9]
- **The pediatricians back it.** Proponents' voter guide statement said sex verification is standard procedure endorsed by the American Academy of Pediatrics. The Guardian reports that the academy has publicly rejected that claim.[^9]

There is also a real distinction on medical exams. The initiative does not use the words "genital exam," and the campaign says none is required.[^2] But it names reproductive anatomy as a basis for verification, and medical professionals warn that implementing it could involve invasive examination or testing.[^9][^11] The exact implementation is contested; the verification requirement itself is in the measure.

Supporters also point to public opinion, and the abstract question is not one-sided: a 2025 Gallup poll found roughly two-thirds of respondents said trans athletes should play on teams matching their sex at birth, according to The Hill.[^12] The Advocate reports that the campaign's own poll showing 56% support came without the full data or methodology needed for independent evaluation.[^11] Voters are not being asked an abstract question, though. They are being asked to approve a medical certification requirement for every girl who wants to play, written to exclude a few.

Girls deserve meaningful opportunities in sports, including safe facilities, qualified coaches, and fair access. Excluding transgender girls and making girls' participation contingent on medical verification is not a defensible way to provide them. **Vote no on I-638.**

## I-001: Parental access and a student's need for privacy

I-001 would undo amendments the Legislature made in 2025 to the state's parental rights statute and restore the version enacted through Initiative 2081 in 2024. The attorney general says it would broaden the records schools must produce, eliminate the statute's exception for a staff member's private memory aid, and require records within 10 business days rather than up to 45 days.[^6] The text would also require schools to notify parents if a child is removed from campus without parental permission, "including to stay at a youth shelter or 'host home,'" and would let parents opt a child out of surveys and student engagements that include questions about "the child's sexual experiences or attractions."[^13]

For a student who is questioning their identity, discussing bullying, or seeking help with a difficult home situation, the possibility that sensitive school information could reach a parent matters. Supportive families are an essential source of care; not every student has the same safety or freedom to share everything at home. No Hate in WA State, citing national data on youth homelessness, argues that LGBTQ+ students rejected by their families face outsized risk.[^14]

Let's Go Washington is correct that I-001 does not say schools must notify parents solely because a student identifies as LGBTQ+.[^2] That does not settle the concern, because the measure widens access to records that can contain such information, including "records of any mental health counseling."[^13]

The campaign also says abusive parents "will not be able to access information about their student records."[^2] The text is narrower than that. Current law shields a student's health care, social work, counseling, or disciplinary records from a parent who is the defendant in a criminal case naming the student as the victim, or who is the target of an abuse or neglect investigation. I-001 would keep a shield only for medical, health, or mental health counseling records, only while an abuse or neglect investigation is pending, and unless the parent has a court order.[^13] The attorney general adds that the right to immediate notification of abuse or misconduct by a school employee or contractor would be removed.[^6]

The attorney general also says I-001 would remove from this statute a clause describing a right to an education free of discrimination on grounds including sexual orientation, gender expression, and gender identity, replacing it with an assurance against discrimination based on the "sincerely held religious beliefs" of a child's family.[^6][^13] That does not mean the initiative repeals every separate nondiscrimination protection in Washington law: the removed clause itself pointed to prohibitions in other chapters of state law.[^13] But the statute's explicit protection for LGBTQ+ students would be gone.

Parents and guardians should be able to support their children without turning schools into a mechanism for exposing students' sensitive information. **Vote no on I-001.**

## Who is behind the measures?

Let's Go Washington calls itself a "non-partisan political action committee."[^2] The Washington State Standard describes it as a "conservative political committee," and OPB headlined its founder a "GOP megadonor."[^3][^15] Heywood is a hedge fund manager who leads the committee.[^4] The Washington State Republican Party, chaired by Rep. **Jim Walsh**, urges a yes vote on all three measures and has endorsed the Republican challenger in the 9th Congressional District.[^16]

The money is concentrated. OPB reported that Heywood spent millions of his own money on the committee's 2024 slate of initiatives.[^15] The committee's own disclosure lists Heywood, Steve Gordon, Washington Rising Inc., Kelley DevCom LLC, and Lawrence Hughes as its top five donors.[^2] The Standard reported that Heywood expects winning the income tax campaign to cost the committee more than $10 million.[^3]

The agenda extends well beyond schools. The Public Disclosure Commission (PDC) says the committee formed in 2022 to back several initiatives, three of which the Legislature enacted and three of which went to the 2024 ballot.[^17] OPB reports that the slate sought to repeal or roll back policies championed by Democrats, including the state's major climate law, its long-term care program, limits on police car chases, and the capital gains tax, and that the committee was instrumental in passing a parents' rights law the Legislature enacted that year.[^15] The current campaign pairs two measures that affect LGBTQ+ students with a repeal of a tax on very high incomes. GLAAD's accountability profile, updated October 2, says the committee drafted and promoted initiatives "targeting LGBTQ people, youth, and families."[^18]

There is also a public record of campaign finance violations. In 2024, the PDC fined Let's Go Washington $20,000, with half suspended on conditions, after finding that it failed to report signature gathering subvendors and failed to produce campaign records when requested. The commission wrote that the committee "has an obligation to inquire and confirm whether its contractors have used subvendors." It did not find a violation on a separate dispute over how signature gathering expenditures were allocated under the rules then in effect.[^17]

A second matter is unresolved. In August, the PDC opened a formal investigation into a complaint from the watchdog group Washingtonians for Ethical Government, which alleges that the committee failed to disclose in-kind advertising by podcaster Brandi Kruse, valued by the complainant at between $345,000 and $1.25 million. A lawyer for the committee denies that any payment or reimbursement was made to Kruse, and the committee has called the complaint a "nothing burger." No finding has been made.[^19]

Opposition has been organized locally and statewide. As of October 27, No Hate in WA State reports that 76 local and tribal governments have passed resolutions against I-001 and I-638, including Issaquah, Renton, Kirkland, Redmond, Bothell, Seattle, and the King County Council, along with the Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, which represents 57 tribal governments. The campaign also lists 405 organizations, among them 39 faith organizations and 33 unions, plus Gov. **Bob Ferguson** and U.S. Sen. **Patty Murray**. The Washington Academy of Family Physicians, the NAACP, the Washington State PTA, the League of Women Voters, the Washington State Nurses Association, and the editorial boards of *The Seattle Times* and *The Columbian* also oppose I-001 and I-638. The organizations No Hate lists in support are few and include Moms for Liberty, XX-XY Athletics, the state Republican Party, and Let's Go Washington itself. These are the campaign's reported tallies, not an independent count.[^20] The Seattle Gay News also urges a no vote on all three measures.[^21]

## I-645: A tax measure with public consequences

I-645 is not a transgender sports or student privacy measure. It belongs in this discussion because Let's Go Washington packaged it with the other two and because it decides whether the state keeps funding the public services LGBTQ+ people and their families rely on.

The initiative would repeal a 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million, scheduled to begin in 2028, and prohibit state and local governments from imposing taxes on individual income or measured by it. The attorney general says repeal would eliminate revenue that would go 95% to the state general fund, which pays for public K–12 education, higher education, health care, and other human services, and 5% to child care and early learning.[^7] The Standard reported the tax could raise a few billion dollars per year once collections begin.[^3]

The campaign warns that lawmakers could lower the threshold.[^2] That is a prediction about what future lawmakers might do, not what the law says. The enacted $1 million deduction applies per household and is adjusted for inflation beginning in 2029.[^7] The campaign also says a yes vote would keep small-business credits and the sales tax exemption on diapers. The attorney general confirms those provisions would remain in place under I-645, but they were never at stake: they stay in law either way.[^2][^7]

Funding schools, health care, and other public services is a community concern, including for LGBTQ+ people who rely on them. Protecting a tax on income above $1 million is a more equitable choice than eliminating that future revenue. **Vote no on I-645.**

## What is on a Newcastle ballot

Newcastle is in Washington's 41st Legislative District, which covers all of Mercer Island and Newcastle along with parts of Bellevue, Renton, Issaquah, and Sammamish.[^22] Newcastle is also in the 9th Congressional District.[^23] Voters should confirm their own districts, because legislative and congressional boundaries do not line up and a neighboring district's candidates are not on every ballot.

**U.S. House, 9th District.** Incumbent Rep. **Adam Smith** (D) faces Republican **Doug Basler**.[^24] In 2025, Smith voted against H.R. 28, the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act, which passed the House 218 to 206.[^25] Its Senate counterpart, S. 9, failed a cloture vote 51 to 45 on March 3, 2025, so it has not become law.[^26] Smith also voted against H.R. 3492, the Protect Children's Innocence Act, which passed the House 216 to 211 and was referred to the Senate Judiciary Committee.[^27][^28] The bill would impose up to 10 years in prison for what it calls "genital or bodily mutilation" and "chemical castration" of a minor, and its definition of the first term includes "any surgery performed for the purpose of changing the body of such individual to correspond to a sex that differs from their biological sex."[^29] Basler's position on these issues has not been established here. The Washington State Republican Party endorsed him and urges a yes vote on all three initiatives, and FOX 13's report on the race focuses on cost of living, taxes, and public safety.[^16][^24] Voters can ask his campaign directly.

**State House, 41st District.** Rep. **Janice Zahn** (D) faces **Elle Nguyen** (R) for Position 1, and Rep. **My-Linh Thai** (D) faces **Michael Rosen**, an independent, for Position 2.[^22] The 2025 law that I-001 would repeal, HB 1296, passed the House 59 to 39 on April 24, 2025, with all 59 Democrats voting yes and all 39 Republicans voting no, so Zahn and Thai, both Democrats, were among the yes votes.[^30][^31] Nguyen lists "public safety, parental rights, protecting children and meaningful local control" among her priorities, and Rosen lists affordability, accountability, and homelessness.[^22] The Issaquah Reporter's candidate profiles do not record a stated position from any of the four on I-001 or I-638. Voters can check the online voters' guide and ask each campaign.

Elsewhere in the state, **Jaelynn Scott**, executive director of the Lavender Rights Project, won more than 88% of the August primary vote in the 37th District in south Seattle and, according to KUOW, is poised to become Washington's first openly transgender legislator. That race is not on Newcastle ballots, but it shows what is possible when trans people are on the ballot as candidates, not just as the subject of a campaign.[^32]

## The national picture

Washington is one of four states with a transgender athlete measure on the ballot. Arizona, Nebraska, and Colorado are the others, and Colorado and Missouri also have measures on gender transition surgeries for minors. Twenty-seven states already restrict transgender athletes by law or agency action, but The Hill and the Movement Advancement Project describe this as the first time voters will decide such policy directly.[^12] Logan Casey of the Movement Advancement Project told The Hill that proponents are turning to the ballot in places like Colorado and Washington after failing to pass restrictions in the legislature.[^12] The Guardian reports that a June 2026 Supreme Court ruling held such bans constitutional.[^9]

The national fight is also being waged inside the Democratic Party, and accountability cannot stop at party affiliation. In Iowa, Democratic Senate candidate **Josh Turek** said in a campaign ad that he opposes "males competing in female sports, something that is already illegal in Iowa," and is "against taxpayers paying for sex changes." LGBTQ+ advocacy group One Iowa Action condemned the ad. Turek also voted against Senate File 418, the 2025 law that removed gender identity from Iowa's civil rights protections. His Republican opponent, Rep. Ashley Hinson, has also regularly spoken and voted against transgender athletes in girls' sports and for other restrictions on transgender medical care, the Dispatch reports. Both facts belong in an honest account of the race.[^33]

Journalist Erin Reed has argued that voters should withhold their votes from the worst anti-trans Democrats, naming Turek among others. Reed says she is not asking anyone to vote Republican or to stay home; that is her recommendation, not a candidate endorsement or a request here to leave a race blank.[^34] Her outlet's election-night guide treats the Michigan, Minnesota, and Ohio Senate races as tests of whether Democrats must abandon trans people to win, and it reports that the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee aired what appears to be its first anti-trans ad, in South Carolina.[^35][^36]

Reed also reported that Representative **Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez**, asked by a transgender student at a Maine town hall about Democrats who have abandoned trans people, called them "wrong" and said, "the moment you compromise the rights of a trans person in America, you compromise the rights of every citizen in America." The exchange shows that candidates within the same party do not all answer these questions the same way.[^36]

Washington is connected to this national fight, but local voters have specific power over the measures on their own ballots. A vote on I-638 and I-001 is a decision about the schools and students in this state, not a symbolic referendum on a national talking point.

## How to vote

Election Day is **Tuesday, November 3, 2026**. Official ballot drop boxes close at **8 p.m.** that day. A mailed ballot must be postmarked by November 3; check the mail collection time before relying on a postmark. The Postal Service recommended mailing by October 27, so an official drop box is a useful option for a voter returning a ballot after that date.[^1]

The deadline for election officials to receive online or mailed registrations and updates was October 26. Eligible voters can still register or update their information and vote in person by 8 p.m. on Election Day. Voting center hours vary.[^1]

Use [VoteWA](https://voter.votewa.gov/) to check your registration and ballot status, find a ballot drop box or voting center, request a replacement ballot, and view your personalized guide.[^1]

Transgender young people deserve teammates, trusted adults, privacy, and the freedom to participate in their communities. Those are not privileges to be traded away for campaign messaging. Washington voters can reject I-638 and I-001, and reject I-645's removal of revenue for public services.
