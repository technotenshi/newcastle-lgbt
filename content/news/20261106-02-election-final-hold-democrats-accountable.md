:Dateline Voters in four states approved bans on transgender girls in girls' school sports on November 3: Washington (I-638), Colorado (Proposition 134), Arizona (Proposition 318) and Nebraska (Measure 442).[^1] Democrats won both chambers of Congress the same night.[^1] Several of the winners had voted or campaigned against trans people. This is our final election-results report: the latest Washington counts, then the Democrats who won after doing it, and what to demand of them.

*Counts as of November 6, 2026: the Washington Secretary of State's 7:09 p.m. PST export, King County's 3:53 p.m. update, Pierce County's 11:48 a.m. update and the Associated Press's 7:22 p.m. seat counts. Results are unofficial until certification.[^2]*

**What changed since our November 5 update:**

- **I-638's lead grew** to 367,243 votes, 56.50% Yes. King County is still rejecting it, 57.81% No.[^2]
- **I-001 and I-645 are still being rejected**, by 118,170 votes (52.14% No) and 435,252 votes (57.67% No).[^2]
- **No Washington contest changed leader.** Our Supreme Court pick Ian Birk still trails Sean O'Donnell, now by 55,588 votes, and Republican Brian Burnett still leads Democrat Stacy Willoughby by 96 votes in the 12th District, Position 1.[^2]
- **Democrats hold 254 U.S. House seats** to Republicans' 162, with 19 uncalled. In the Senate, Democrats and independents who caucus with them hold 54 seats, Republicans 44, with two uncalled. Governors are unchanged at 28 Democrats, 20 Republicans and two uncalled.[^3][^4][^5]

## Four states took trans girls off girls' teams

Washington's I-638 passed with more than 56% of the vote. Colorado's Proposition 134, supported by 52%, requires schools and athletic associations to assign sports as male, female or coed and limits athletes to the sex they were assigned at birth. Arizona's Proposition 318 bars trans girls from girls' sports at every school and athletic association, restricts bathrooms and other private spaces, and lets athletes sue schools that break the rules. Nebraska's Measure 442 wrote its ban into the state constitution.[^1]

AP noted that Washington and Colorado voted after Democratic-led governors' offices and legislatures declined to enact such bans.[^1] Voters in Missouri did reject Amendment 3, which would have banned gender-transition treatment for minors and repealed the right to abortion.[^1] Washington voters are rejecting I-001, the attack on student privacy.[^2]

**Sheriff Keith Swank** organized the anti-trans demonstration at the November 1 Reign match. *The News Tribune* reported emails in which he told a group of 87 people to buy tickets and reveal XX-XY Athletics shirts at halftime, and quoted him: "I posted that we should do this, and I organized it, just like I said I would."[^15] Over social-media posts disparaging transgender people that he made as a Seattle police captain, a Seattle Police Department disciplinary report sustained findings against him for bias-free policing, professionalism and social-media violations, and concluded: "If you were still a Department employee, I would have terminated your employment."[^16] [Our Reign report](/news/2026/11/02/sheriff-anti-trans-protest-reign-match) has the full record.

**Amendment 53** would make the sheriff appointed by the county executive and confirmed by a Council majority, ending Swank's elected term on January 1, 2027.[^17] It is the one measure on the ballot that would have removed him early, and it is losing by roughly two to one. Swank serves out his term.

**Amendment 54** would create a Public Safety Ombuds department for independent oversight of the sheriff and his employees, subject to legal limits. It would publish findings and recommendations, refer complaints to appropriate authorities and establish a community advisory committee.[^18] Voters are rejecting it too, 51.86% to 48.14%.[^14] Pierce County keeps a sheriff with a documented anti-trans record and no new independent office to watch his department.

Trans residents of Pierce County, and the trans fans and players who travel to Lumen Field, deserve policing that treats them as people to protect, not targets to organize against. That work continues through complaints, public records and the next sheriff election.

## Democrats who won after voting or campaigning against trans people

Control of Congress is a means. Here is what the winners did on the record.

### Washington: Perez and Schrier

Reps. **Marie Gluesenkamp Perez** (WA-3) and **Kim Schrier** (WA-8) are both counted among Washington's eight Democratic U.S. House wins, with 53.66% and 56.19% of the official count.[^22][^2] On September 10, 2025, each voted for Rep. Nancy Mace's amendment barring trans women from women's teams at the military academies. On December 10, 2025, each voted for the defense bill that made it law.[^23][^24][^25] On May 20, 2026, Perez also voted for H.R. 2616, which would make elementary and middle schools that receive federal education funds get a parent's consent before changing a student's pronouns or name on any school form, or the student's access to locker rooms or bathrooms. Schrier voted against it.[^26][^27] [Our September report](/news/2026/09/08/perez-schrier-anti-trans-votes-cost) has the full record.

### More House Democrats cast the same votes

All 10 also voted Yea on the final defense bill, which passed 312 to 112.[^23][^24] That day the House also voted on five more anti-trans amendments to the bill: Rep. Ralph Norman's (No. 13), four more from Mace (Nos. 14 to 17) and one from Rep. Cory Mills (No. 18).[^28][^29][^30][^31][^32][^33] Cuellar and Davis voted Aye on all five, and Gray on four. Gonzalez and Figures did not vote for the academy ban but did vote for others, as the table shows. Eleven of these 12 members ran on November 3 and won. The twelfth, Rep. Jared Golden, did not run.

### On the campaign trail: Senate, governor and House winners

Votes are one record. Campaign messages are another, and many winners distanced themselves from trans people in their own words. These are the ones we verified in a news report that quotes or describes the message.

**James Talarico** won the Texas Senate seat.[^47] As a state representative he voted against Republican restrictions on trans student-athletes. On September 9 he told Fox News: "I will oppose trans athletes playing in girls' sports if it risks safety or fairness," which *The Texas Tribune* called a reversal.[^48] AP reported that he also said he would vote for a national ban on gender-affirming surgeries for transgender minors.[^49] In 2025 his campaign told Stonewall Democrats of Austin that "if elected, Rep. Talarico would sponsor the Equality Act in the U.S. Senate." After Republicans attacked his record, a spokesperson told *The Washington Post* only that he "will review every bill he's asked to vote on."[^50]

**Josh Turek** won the Iowa Senate seat.[^51] In an October 25 ad he said: "It's why I oppose males competing in female sports, something that is already illegal in Iowa. I'm against taxpayers paying for sex changes too."[^52] *Iowa Capital Dispatch* reported that he supported blocking Medicaid funding for gender-affirming care for transgender Iowans, and that One Iowa Action "unequivocally" condemned the ad.[^52]

**Annie Andrews** won the South Carolina Senate seat.[^53] On October 8, days after President Trump called her an anti-trans slur on social media, she released a direct-to-camera ad in a lab coat. She said: "I don't support biological men playing in women's sports, and I don't support and haven't ever performed gender surgery for minors."[^54][^55] Those are her words, and "biological men" is her phrase, not ours. Twenty South Carolina LGBTQ+ groups signed an open letter denouncing "transphobic attacks, slurs, and fear-based political messages" in the state's midterm races.[^55] Human Rights Campaign President Kelley Robinson, responding to Democrats who have adopted more conservative framing on trans issues, said: "You don't have to sacrifice any member of our community to win in these races, to have a message that resonates with voters."[^56]

**Rob Sand** won the Iowa governorship.[^51] The *Capital Dispatch* reported that he has repeatedly said he does not support allowing transgender women to compete in women's sports, and AP reported he has spoken against it as a matter of fairness.[^52][^49] When a conservative radio host asked him in May 2025 whether "biological males" should play women's sports, he answered "no," The 19th reported. Sand has also said it was "wrong" for Iowa Republicans to repeal civil rights protections for trans people.[^57] In August he said: "Girls' sports are for girls, and boys' sports are for boys."[^55]

**Amy Acton** won the Ohio governorship.[^58] On June 30, after the Supreme Court upheld state bans, she told the Toledo newspaper: "I do not support boys playing in girls sports. This is already settled law in Ohio, and as governor I will enforce and uphold the law." Trans girls are girls. The 19th reported that Ohio LGBTQ+ leaders wanted more from her and that "no public or private reassurances came."[^57] Equality Ohio, one of the state's largest LGBTQ+ advocacy groups, changed her 2026 candidate scorecard status from an "A" to "pending."[^55]

**Aaron Ford** won the Nevada governorship.[^59] He told a Nevada newspaper: "I personally do not support trans athletes competing in sports opposite their gender assigned at birth."[^57]

**Xavier Becerra** won the California governorship.[^60] In a September CBS News interview he said: "There's nothing in the Constitution that says that you are entitled to play a sport." He had not realized California law already guarantees trans students the right to play. After the interviewer read him the law, he said: "I'm not interested as governor in having someone be bullied or someone discriminated because of who they are, simply for that reason. And if the rules allow an individual to play in that sport, those are the rules and you should abide by them."[^61] His first answer is the one Erin Reed flagged. His second, after he learned the law, is the one he should be held to.

**Adam Hamilton** won the Kansas Senate seat from Sen. Roger Marshall.[^62] PolitiFact reported that his campaign spokesperson, Tyson Brody, said Hamilton does not think girls should "compete against boys" and that gender-affirming surgeries "shouldn't be performed on children."[^63] Those are the spokesperson's words, not a direct quote from Hamilton. PolitiFact also found that a Republican ad attacking Hamilton on trans issues stitched his remarks together misleadingly.

**Sarah Trone Garriott** flipped Iowa's 3rd Congressional District.[^64] Her campaign video said: "Politicians have become too extreme on these issues. I'll always put kids and families first." Political.org described it as "staking out opposition to men in women's locker rooms." As an Iowa state senator she had voted against the state's 2022 sports ban, its 2023 ban on gender-affirming care for minors and its 2023 school bathroom bill, and critics say the video contradicts that record.[^65]

**Abdul El-Sayed** took the opposite approach and won the Michigan Senate seat.[^66] Discussing trans athletes on *60 Minutes* days before the election, he said: "I think we manufacture these social issues." He added that he is "not running to govern sports," the *New York Post* reported.[^67]

Three more House winners campaigned against trans girls in their own ads. Rep. **Marcy Kaptur** (OH-9) aired an ad saying she "voted with Republicans to keep transgender ideology out of our schools" and that "boys should not play in girls' sports." Equality PAC, the fundraising arm of the Congressional Equality Caucus, called her language "unacceptable" and said it was "absolutely not supporting her campaign at this time."[^54] Rep. **Don Davis** (NC-1) said in an ad: "There's real concerns about fairness and safety; that's what I believe states should decide what's right for protecting women and girls."[^54] Rep. **Kathy Castor** (FL-14) said she did not believe "biological males" should take part in girls' sports, *The Guardian* reported. *The Guardian* lists Kaptur and Castor among the Democrats who echoed anti-trans messaging and won.[^68]

**Sherrod Brown** won the Ohio Senate seat from Sen. Jon Husted.[^69] When Republicans attacked him on trans issues, he answered with an ad of voters dismissing the attacks and turned to costs. The Human Rights Campaign said he "didn't play into MAGA's hands."[^54] Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear, asked on CNN about Democrats who say trans girls should not play on girls' teams, said: "We don't throw anybody under the bus. That is not the party that we are."[^70]

A Democrat who says trans girls should be kept off girls' teams is making the argument the ban's backers made. These statements did not write I-638, and we cannot say they moved a vote. They are on the record anyway, from the people who will now vote on federal policy and run state governments.

They also were not needed to win. Republicans spent an estimated $183 million on anti-trans ads this cycle, according to AP's count of AdImpact data.[^71] Dade Phelan, the former Republican speaker of the Texas House, said in October: "We're still running transgender ads. It's just not resonating. $100 million in ads, $125 million in ads, and nothing's moved the needle here in Texas in a month in the Senate race."[^72] After the election, Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis, a Republican who listed Florida's bans on trans girls in girls' sports and on gender-affirming care for minors among his policies, said: "I knew Republicans were in trouble when I was reading an overview about what they were running ads on, and they're spending hundreds of millions on transgender ads."[^71] Texas, Kansas, the U.S. Senate and the U.S. House all flipped to Democrats, *The Guardian* reported.[^68] Andrew R. Flores, an American University professor, told *The Guardian*: "Instead of guiding the public toward an attitude that could be more accepting, some Democrats capitulated." Zein Murib, a Fordham University political scientist, said of El-Sayed: "He wasn't going to throw trans people under the bus."[^68] El-Sayed's win, and Brown's, show that a Democrat could answer differently and win.

### Democratic-run states ended Medicaid coverage for trans youth

Some of the harm is coming from Democrats who hold executive power now. A federal rule that took effect October 13 bars federal Medicaid and CHIP money from paying for gender-affirming care for minors, and Democratic attorneys general sued over it. Several Democrat-led states, including California, said they would keep the care running on state money while the case proceeds. Virginia and Arizona did not. Virginia's Department of Medical Assistance Services said it "will no longer cover gender dysphoria services for Medicaid members under the age of 18, and CHIP members under the age of 19."[^73] Colorado's Medicaid agency said it would suspend coverage for hormone therapy and other gender-affirming care for youth on the same date, and that "any ability to resume covering gender affirming care will require legislative action."[^74]

That legislature is now Democratic. *The Colorado Sun* reported that Democrats won what appear to be supermajorities in both chambers and that Gov.-elect Phil Weiser, a Democrat, is already discussing changes to the voter-approved anti-trans ballot measures.[^75] Weiser, Gov. Katie Hobbs of Arizona and Gov. Abigail Spanberger of Virginia can fund this care with state dollars and say so, as other Democratic-led states did. Ending coverage for hundreds of trans youth is a choice by officials who campaigned as their allies.

### Washington's party leadership wavered on I-638

Shasti Conrad, chair of the Washington State Democratic Party, told KUOW on Wednesday she planned to fight the ban in court. On Thursday morning the party told KUOW it did not plan to litigate.[^76] Trans kids needed the party to hold its position.
