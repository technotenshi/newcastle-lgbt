:Dateline Voters in four states approved measures restricting transgender athletes on November 3: Washington (I-638), Colorado (Proposition 134), Arizona (Proposition 318) and Nebraska (Measure 442). Arizona expanded an existing ban, and Nebraska put one in its constitution.[^1] Democrats won both chambers of Congress the same night.[^2] Several of the winners had voted or campaigned against trans people. This is our final election-results report: the latest Washington counts, then the Democrats who won after doing it, and what to demand of them.

*Counts as of November 6, 2026, unofficial until certification, except the Washington Senate scenario chart, which is the Bellevue Chamber's November 4 count.[^3]*

**What changed since our [November 4 article, updated November 5](/news/2026/11/04/election-results-lgbtq-washington):**

- **I-638's lead grew** to 367,243 votes, 56.50% Yes. King County is still rejecting it, 57.81% No.[^3]
- **I-001 and I-645 are still being rejected**, by 118,170 votes (52.14% No) and 435,252 votes (57.67% No).[^3]
- **No Washington contest changed leader.** Our Supreme Court pick Ian Birk still trails Sean O'Donnell, now by 55,588 votes, and Republican Brian Burnett still leads Democrat Stacy Willoughby by 96 votes in the 12th District, Position 1.[^3]
- **Democrats hold 254 U.S. House seats** to Republicans' 162, with 19 uncalled. In the Senate, Democrats and independents who caucus with them hold 54 seats, Republicans 44, with two uncalled. Governors are unchanged at 28 Democrats, 20 Republicans and two uncalled.[^4][^5][^6]

## Four states voted to restrict trans athletes

Washington's I-638 passed with more than 56% of the vote. Colorado's Proposition 134, supported by 52%, requires school and athletic association teams to be designated for men or boys, women or girls, or as mixed, and excludes trans girls from girls' teams. Mixed teams remain open to all students.[^1][^7] Arizona's Proposition 318 extends a ban that already covered public schools to every school and athletic association, restricts bathrooms and other private spaces connected to athletic participation, and lets athletes sue schools that break the rules. Nebraska, which already had a ban, wrote it into the state constitution with Measure 442.[^1] Colorado's Proposition 135, which bans certain gender-related surgeries for minors, was passing with 52% of the vote on November 6 and its opponents had conceded, The Colorado Sun reported. AP had not called it as of 9 a.m. that day.[^8]

AP noted that Washington and Colorado voted after Democratic-led governors' offices and legislatures declined to enact such bans.[^1] Voters in Missouri did reject Amendment 3, which would have banned gender-transition treatment for minors and repealed the right to abortion.[^1] Washington voters are rejecting I-001, the attack on student privacy.[^3]

## Jaelynn Scott: a trans woman in the Legislature as trans students are targeted

The official count gives **Jaelynn Scott** 44,490 votes, or 87.49%, against **Evon McCorkle's** 5,975, or 11.75%, with 389 write-ins, in the 37th District, Position 2. Her lead is 38,515 votes.[^3] LGBTQ Nation reports that she has won and describes her as Washington's first trans legislator and the first out Black trans woman elected to serve in a U.S. state legislature.[^10]

The timing matters. In the same election that approved a ban on trans girls in girls' sports, 37th District voters gave a trans woman 87% of the vote. As the Legislature, school districts and OSPI respond to I-638, trans Washingtonians will have one of their own in the room, not just allies speaking about them. Our [earlier profile](/news/2026/10/28/jaelynn-scott-first-openly-trans-legislator) explains the work and priorities behind the candidacy.

Stating her milestone precisely honors the trans people who came before her. The words **openly**, **woman** and **state legislature** matter. Rutgers records that Althea Garrison, a Black trans woman, was elected to the Massachusetts House in 1992 and was outed against her wishes after the election.[^11] Mo Turner, who is Black and nonbinary, previously served in Oklahoma's legislature.[^12] Andrea Jenkins became the first openly transgender African American woman elected to U.S. public office in 2017, at the Minneapolis City Council level.[^13] Scott's narrower distinction must not erase those predecessors.

**Sheriff Keith Swank** organized the anti-trans demonstration at the November 1 Reign match. *The News Tribune* reported emails in which he told a group of 87 people to buy tickets and reveal XX-XY Athletics shirts at halftime, and quoted him: "I posted that we should do this, and I organized it, just like I said I would."[^24] Over social-media posts disparaging transgender people that he made as a Seattle police captain, a Seattle Police Department disciplinary report sustained findings against him for bias-free policing, professionalism and social-media violations, and concluded: "If you were still a Department employee, I would have terminated your employment."[^25][^26] [Our Reign report](/news/2026/11/02/sheriff-anti-trans-protest-reign-match) has the full record.

Trans residents of Pierce County, and the trans fans and players who travel to Lumen Field, deserve policing that treats them as people to protect, not targets to organize against. If these results hold, that work continues through complaints, public records and the next sheriff election.

## Democrats who won after voting or campaigning against trans people

Here is what the winners did on the record.

### Washington: Perez and Schrier

Reps. **Marie Gluesenkamp Perez** (WA-3) and **Kim Schrier** (WA-8) are both counted among Washington's eight Democratic U.S. House wins, with 53.62% and 56.19% of the official count.[^14][^3] On September 10, 2025, each voted for Rep. Nancy Mace's amendment barring trans women from women's teams at the military academies. On December 10, 2025, each voted for the defense bill that made it law.[^31][^32][^33] On May 20, 2026, Perez also voted for H.R. 2616, which would require public schools receiving funds under the Elementary and Secondary Education Act to obtain a parent's consent before changing a minor elementary or middle school student's pronouns or name on a school form, or sex-based accommodations such as locker rooms or bathrooms. The bill also prohibits using those federal education funds "to teach or advance concepts related to gender ideology," the bill's wording. Schrier voted against it.[^34][^35] [Our September report](/news/2026/09/08/perez-schrier-anti-trans-votes-cost) has the full record.

### More House Democrats cast the same votes

All 10 also voted Yea on the final defense bill, which passed 312 to 112.[^31][^32] On September 10, the House also voted on five more amendments: four restricting trans people's access to care or facilities or excluding gender identity from forms, and one restricting flags. These were Rep. Ralph Norman's (No. 13), three more from Mace (Nos. 14, 16 and 17) and one from Rep. Cory Mills (No. 18).[^36][^37][^38][^39][^40][^41] Cuellar and Davis voted Aye on all five, and Gray on four. The votes continued this year. On July 21, 2026, Figures, Rep. Terri Sewell (AL-7), Cuellar, Davis and Gonzalez were the five Democrats who voted for a Mace amendment barring TRICARE from covering gender-related care, which passed 219 to 208, and for a second Mace amendment barring trans girls from girls' sports in Defense Department schools, which passed 221 to 203. Perez and Schrier voted No on both.[^53][^54][^55] Sewell won re-election with 69.4%.[^56]

### On the campaign trail: Senate, governor and House winners

Votes are one record. Campaign messages are another, and many winners distanced themselves from trans people in their own words. These are the ones we verified in a news report that quotes or describes the message.

**James Talarico** won the Texas Senate seat.[^58] As a state representative he voted against Republican restrictions on trans student-athletes. On September 9 he told Fox News: "I will oppose trans athletes playing in girls' sports if it risks safety or fairness," which *The Texas Tribune* called a reversal.[^59] AP reported that he also said he would vote for a national ban on gender-affirming surgeries for transgender minors.[^60] In 2025 his campaign told Stonewall Democrats of Austin that "if elected, Rep. Talarico would sponsor the Equality Act in the U.S. Senate." After Republicans attacked his record, campaign spokesperson JT Ennis told *The Washington Post*: "If elected, James will review every bill he's asked to vote on and do what's best for the people of Texas."[^61]

**Josh Turek** won the Iowa Senate seat.[^62] In an October 25 ad he said: "It's why I oppose males competing in female sports, something that is already illegal in Iowa. I'm against taxpayers paying for sex changes too."[^63] *Iowa Capital Dispatch* reported that he supported blocking Medicaid funding for gender-affirming care for transgender Iowans, and that One Iowa Action "unequivocally" condemned the ad.[^63]

**Annie Andrews** won the South Carolina Senate seat.[^64] On October 8, days after President Trump called her an anti-trans slur on social media, she released a direct-to-camera ad in a lab coat. She said: "I don't support biological men playing in women's sports, and I don't support and haven't ever performed gender surgery for minors."[^65][^66] Those are her words, and "biological men" is her phrase, not ours. Twenty South Carolina LGBTQ+ groups signed an open letter denouncing "transphobic attacks, slurs, and fear-based political messages" in the state's midterm races.[^66] Human Rights Campaign President Kelley Robinson, responding to Democrats who have adopted more conservative framing on trans issues, said: "You don't have to sacrifice any member of our community to win in these races to have a message that resonates with voters."[^67]

**Rob Sand** won the Iowa governorship.[^62] The *Capital Dispatch* reported that he has repeatedly said he does not support allowing transgender women to compete in women's sports, and AP reported he has spoken against it as a matter of fairness.[^63][^60] The 19th reported that when a conservative radio host asked him in May 2025 whether trans women should play women's sports, he answered "no." Sand has also said it was "wrong" for Iowa Republicans to repeal civil rights protections for trans people.[^68] In August he said: "Girls' sports are for girls, and boys' sports are for boys."[^66]

**Amy Acton** won the Ohio governorship.[^69] On June 30, after the Supreme Court upheld state bans, she told the Toledo newspaper: "I do not support boys playing in girls sports. This is already settled law in Ohio, and as governor I will enforce and uphold the law." Trans girls are girls. The 19th reported that Ohio LGBTQ+ leaders wanted more from her and that she offered no reassurance, public or private.[^68] Equality Ohio, one of the state's largest LGBTQ+ advocacy groups, changed her 2026 candidate scorecard status from an "A" to "pending."[^66]

**Aaron Ford** won the Nevada governorship.[^70] He told a Nevada newspaper: "I personally do not support trans athletes competing in sports opposite their gender assigned at birth."[^68]

**Xavier Becerra** won the California governorship.[^71] In a September 2025 CBS News interview he said: "There's nothing in the Constitution that says that you are entitled to play a sport." CBS reported he was seemingly unaware that California law already guarantees trans students the right to play. After the interviewer read him the law, CBS reported that he ultimately supported it: "I'm not interested as governor in having someone be bullied or someone discriminated because of who they are, simply for that reason," Becerra said. "And if the rules allow an individual to play in that sport, those are the rules and you should abide by them."[^72] His second answer, after the law was read to him, is the one he should be held to.

**Adam Hamilton** won the Kansas Senate seat from Sen. Roger Marshall.[^73] PolitiFact reported that his campaign spokesperson, Tyson Brody, said Hamilton does not think girls should "compete against boys" and that gender-affirming surgeries should not be performed on children. Brody added: "But no one should attack or demean a small and vulnerable group of people just to score political points." This is the campaign's account, not a direct quote from Hamilton. PolitiFact also found that a Republican ad attacking Hamilton on trans issues stitched his remarks together misleadingly.[^74]

**Sarah Trone Garriott** flipped Iowa's 3rd Congressional District.[^75] The 19th reported that in an August campaign ad she said she opposes "letting men in women's locker rooms."[^66] That rhetoric distances her from trans people despite her Iowa Senate votes against a 2022 trans sports ban and 2023 bans on youth gender-affirming care and access to school bathrooms.[^76][^77]

**Abdul El-Sayed** took the opposite approach and won the Michigan Senate seat.[^78] Discussing trans athletes on *60 Minutes* days before the election, he said: "I think we manufacture these social issues." He added that he is "not running to govern sports," the *New York Post* reported.[^79]

Four House winners also have relevant campaign statements or votes. Rep. **Marcy Kaptur** (OH-9) aired an ad saying she "voted with Republicans to keep transgender ideology out of our schools" and that "boys should not play in girls' sports." **Sherrod Brown** won the Ohio Senate seat from Sen. Jon Husted.[^83] When Republicans attacked him on trans issues, he answered with an ad of voters dismissing the attacks and turned to costs. The Human Rights Campaign said he "didn't play into MAGA's hands."[^65] Brown is not a clean contrast: in a 2024 ad responding to Republican attacks, his campaign highlighted Ohio's existing ban on trans girls in sports and included a WKYC fact-check denying that Brown had voted to allow trans women in women's sports. The ad said Brown agreed with Gov. Mike DeWine that local sports leagues, not politicians, should make those decisions. He also said "sports need to be fair and there should be no unfair advantage," but that Kentucky's athletic association already had rules in place.[^85]

A Democrat who says trans girls should be kept off girls' teams is making the argument the ban's backers made. These statements did not write I-638, and we cannot say they moved a vote. They are on the record anyway, from the people who will now vote on federal policy and run state governments.

The Republican attacks these Democrats were answering did not save Republicans. Republicans spent an estimated $183 million on anti-trans television ads through October 25, according to AdImpact data reported by AP.[^86] Dade Phelan, the former Republican speaker of the Texas House, told *Texas Monthly* in October, as the *Houston Chronicle* reported: "We're still running transgender ads. It's just not resonating. $100 million in ads, $125 million in ads, and nothing's moved the needle here in Texas in a month in the Senate race."[^87] After the election, Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis, a Republican who listed Florida's bans on trans girls in girls' sports and on gender-affirming care for minors among his policies, said he knew Republicans were in trouble after reading an overview of their advertising and seeing the hundreds of millions of dollars spent on anti-trans ads, according to *Advocate*.[^88] Texas, Kansas, the U.S. Senate and the U.S. House all flipped to Democrats, *The Guardian* reported.[^81] Andrew R. Flores, an American University professor, told *The Guardian*: "Instead of guiding the public toward an attitude that could be more accepting, some Democrats capitulated." Zein Murib, a Fordham University political scientist, said of El-Sayed: "He wasn't going to throw trans people under the bus."[^81] El-Sayed's win shows that a Democrat could answer differently and win.

### Democratic governors' Medicaid agencies cut coverage for trans youth

A federal rule effective October 13 restricts federal Medicaid funding for specified gender-affirming medications and surgeries for enrollees under 18, and separate CHIP funding for those under 19. It permits up to six months of federal matching for eligible patients already receiving hormone therapy, but not for puberty blockers or surgery; mental health services remain eligible for federal funding.[^89] Democratic attorneys general sued over the rule.[^90] Several Democrat-led states, including California, said they would keep the care running on state money while the case proceeds. Virginia and Arizona did not. Under Gov. Abigail Spanberger, Virginia's Department of Medical Assistance Services said it "will no longer cover gender dysphoria services for Medicaid members under the age of 18, and CHIP members under the age of 19," while specifying that mental health coverage remains unchanged.[^91] Under Gov. Katie Hobbs, Arizona updated its prior authorization criteria to exclude specified testosterone products and puberty blockers used for gender dysphoria in patients under 19, with a six-month exception for patients who started the requested drug on or before October 13.[^90][^92] Colorado's Medicaid agency, under outgoing Gov. Jared Polis,[^93] said it would suspend coverage for hormone therapy and other gender-affirming care for youth on the same date, and that "any ability to resume covering gender affirming care will require legislative action." About 500 people under 19 in Colorado received Medicaid-covered hormone therapy in the 2024-25 fiscal year.[^94]

Democrats control Colorado's legislature. *The Colorado Sun* reported that Democrats won what appear to be supermajorities in both chambers and that Gov.-elect Phil Weiser, a Democrat, is already discussing changes to the anti-trans ballot measures, which the legislature can amend or overturn by a simple majority vote.[^95] Arizona had a Republican legislature when coverage was cut.[^90] Democrats flipped both chambers in this election, which will give Hobbs a Democratic legislature when it convenes in January.[^30] Weiser, Hobbs and Spanberger can ask their legislatures for state money and say so publicly, as other Democratic-led states have kept this care running.
