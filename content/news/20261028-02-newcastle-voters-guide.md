:Dateline Ballots for the November 3 general election are due in an official drop box by 8 p.m., and Newcastle voters will choose a U.S. representative, two state representatives, five state Supreme Court justices, county officials, and judges, and decide three statewide initiatives.[^1]

This guide recommends a vote in each of those races and shows who else backs each choice. Newcastle is in Washington's 41st Legislative District[^2][^3] and the 9th Congressional District,[^4] and King County's district court map places it in the Northeast Electoral District.[^5] Boundaries can change block by block, so check your own ballot or [VoteWA](https://voter.votewa.gov/) before relying on any race here.[^1]

## How we chose

We weighed each candidate's documented record on LGBTQ+ rights: roll-call votes, their own words in questionnaires and the state voters' pamphlet, and, where a source ties them to it, who is backing them. We then checked those findings against four endorsers: Fuse Washington's Progressive Voters Guide,[^6] King County Democrats,[^7] the Stranger's Election Control Board,[^8] and the Seattle Times editorial board.[^9] Where a race has no documented LGBTQ+ record to weigh, we say so and follow the endorsers. Equal Rights Washington says it is not making endorsements this cycle.[^10] Some Seattle Times pages cited below may require a subscription.

## Statewide initiatives: vote no on all three

All three initiatives are sponsored by Let's Go Washington, the political committee led by hedge fund manager **Brian Heywood**.[^14] The state Public Disclosure Commission fined the committee $20,000 in 2024, with half suspended on conditions, for campaign finance violations.[^15] In August it opened a formal investigation into a separate complaint, and no finding has been made.[^16] Our full analysis is in [Washington Voters Face a Choice on Transgender Rights and Student Privacy](/news/2026/10/28/washington-election-transgender-rights). In short:

- **Initiative 638: No.** The attorney general says it would generally prevent transgender girls from playing on girls' sports teams, and that students "would thus have to undergo some type of medical evaluation of their sex."[^17]
- **Initiative 001: No.** The attorney general says it would remove from the parents' rights statute a clause describing a right to an education free of discrimination on grounds including sexual orientation, gender expression, and gender identity.[^18]
- **Initiative 645: No.** This is a tax measure, not an LGBTQ+ one. The attorney general says repealing the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million would eliminate revenue that would go 95% to the state general fund, which pays for K–12 education, higher education, health care, and other human services, and 5% to child care and early learning.[^19]

## U.S. House, 9th District: Adam Smith

Incumbent Rep. **Adam Smith** (D) faces Republican **Doug Basler**. On the votes that most directly concern transgender people, Smith voted against H.R. 28, the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act, which passed the House 218 to 206 in January 2025,[^20] and against H.R. 3492, the Protect Children's Innocence Act, which passed 216 to 211 in December 2025.[^21] The second bill would impose up to 10 years in prison for what it calls "genital or bodily mutilation" and "chemical castration" of a minor.[^22] Fuse says Smith's votes to protect gender-affirming care for transgender youth, among other issues, earned him a 100% Planned Parenthood score in 2025.[^6]

The endorsements come with conditions. The Stranger calls its endorsement reluctant, says the only reason it can offer is that a Democratic House majority could "gum up some of the works" of a second Trump administration, and cites more than $325,000 from AIPAC in this year's primary cycle and $124,051 from Palantir in recent years.[^8] Fuse recommends him but asks him to reconsider AIPAC donations and to revise some foreign policy stances.[^6] The Seattle Times endorses him for his foreign policy expertise and the likelihood that he would chair the House Armed Services Committee if Democrats win the House.[^23]

Basler's voters' pamphlet statement focuses on affordability, the Climate Commitment Act, and public safety, and it does not mention LGBTQ+ rights.[^24] The Washington State Republican Party has endorsed him and urges a yes vote on all three initiatives.[^25] His positions on these issues are not established in the sources we reviewed; voters can ask his campaign. We recommend **Smith**: he has a documented record of voting against both anti-trans bills, and the donor concerns above are reasons to hold him to account after the election, not reasons to leave the race blank.

## 41st Legislative District

### Position 1: Janice Zahn

Rep. **Janice Zahn** (D) faces **Elle Nguyen** (R). HB 1296 is the 2025 law whose amendments to the parents' rights statute I-001 would undo.[^18] The House's final vote shows all 59 Democrats voting yes and all 39 Republicans voting no, so Zahn, a Democrat, voted yes.[^26][^27] Her voters' pamphlet statement lists endorsements from Planned Parenthood, teachers, nurses, and Bellevue, Mercer Island, Issaquah, Sammamish, and Newcastle leaders.[^24]

Nguyen's pamphlet statement promises "traditional values" and "protecting children," and the Issaquah Reporter lists "public safety, parental rights, protecting children and meaningful local control" among her priorities.[^24][^2] The Stranger says her platform shows "subtextual support" for Let's Go Washington's Parents' Bill of Rights.[^8] None of the sources we reviewed records a stated position from Nguyen on I-001 or I-638. We recommend **Zahn**.

### Position 2: My-Linh Thai

Rep. **My-Linh Thai** (D), also a Democrat, voted yes on the same bill.[^26][^27] She sits on the House Civil Rights & Judiciary Committee, and her pamphlet statement commits her to "a more just, inclusive, and equitable society for all."[^24]

Her opponent, **Michael Rosen**, is an independent. His pamphlet statement and campaign website center on accountability, affordability, and health; neither states a position on LGBTQ+ rights or the initiatives.[^24][^28] Fuse says his platform "does not inspire confidence" that he would be a progressive advocate, and The Stranger says he did not respond to its questions.[^6][^8] The Seattle Times endorses Rosen on budget grounds and reports that he calls himself a "pretty reliable" Democratic voter with "progressive social values."[^29] Those are his words as reported by the Times; nothing in the sources we reviewed shows how he would vote on bills that affect LGBTQ+ residents. We recommend **Thai**, whose record is documented, over a candidate whose record on these questions is not.

## State Supreme Court

Five seats are on the ballot, in nonpartisan races.[^24]

- **Position 1: Colleen Melody.** Her voters' pamphlet statement says she led the attorney general's Civil Rights Division,[^24] and Fuse says her work there included protecting LGBTQ+ rights.[^30] Her opponent, tax attorney Scott Edwards, is described by The Stranger as backed by Full Court Press, which it calls former Attorney General Rob McKenna's effort to turn the court conservative.[^8]
- **Position 3: Jaime Hawk.** The King County Superior Court judge and former ACLU attorney is backed by all four endorsers; the Times does so with reservations.[^31] Her opponent, **David Stevens**, insisted in a candidate pledge that marriage is between one man and one woman, according to Fuse,[^30] and told conservative radio host Jason Rantz that Clarence Thomas, Antonin Scalia, and Samuel Alito are his favorite justices, according to the Times.[^31]
- **Position 4: Ian Birk.** Nothing in the sources we reviewed shows an anti-LGBTQ+ position by either Birk or his opponent, Sean O'Donnell, so this recommendation rests on the endorsers. Fuse and The Stranger back Birk and both point to support for O'Donnell from former Republican Attorney General Rob McKenna.[^6][^8] The Seattle Times calls both candidates excellent, endorses O'Donnell, and notes his endorsements span the political spectrum.[^32] This is one of two races where the Times disagrees with the other three endorsers.
- **Position 5: Theo Angelis.** This is the race with the clearest LGBTQ+ stakes on the court ballot. Asked in an iVoterGuide questionnaire whether *Obergefell v. Hodges*, the same-sex marriage decision, was rightly decided, **Dave Larson** answered: "The U.S. Constitution does not regulate marriage."[^33] On *Bostock v. Clayton County*, a ruling on employment discrimination, he wrote that the Court "should not have extended the statute beyond its plain meaning."[^33] The Stranger reports that in its interview Larson said the Legislature, not the courts, should decide civil matters like gay marriage, and that Angelis answered that the Court should protect people's rights because legislators cannot always be trusted to.[^8] Fuse says Larson's campaign has drawn support from Family Policy Institute Washington Action, which it describes as opposing same-sex marriage.[^30] Angelis, appointed by Gov. Bob Ferguson, says in his pamphlet statement that he has fought for "the rights of all individuals."[^24]
- **Position 7: Debra Stephens.** The chief justice faces Todd Bloom.[^24] She is backed by all four endorsers, and the sources we reviewed document no LGBTQ+-specific record in this race, so our recommendation follows the endorsers.[^6][^7][^8][^9]

## King County offices and local courts

**Prosecuting Attorney and Director of Elections.** Leesa Manion and Julie Wise are unopposed, and both are backed by Fuse and King County Democrats.[^6][^7]

**Assessor.** Rob Foxcurran faces Dominique Scarimbolo. Fuse notes that the King County Republican Party recommends Scarimbolo, and all four endorsers back Foxcurran.[^6] No LGBTQ+ record is documented in this race.

**Courts.** Superior Court judges Kent Liu, Daniel York, and Tenaya Scheinman are unopposed and endorsed by King County Democrats.[^12][^7] Court of Appeals judges David Mann and Bill Bowman are also unopposed.[^24] In the Northeast District Court race for Position 1, Jan Trasen, a former public defender and administrative law judge, faces Bianca Tse, a former King County deputy prosecutor who now serves as a pro tem judge; King County Democrats and the Stranger back Trasen.[^11][^8] The other six Northeast District Court positions each list one candidate.[^13]

## How to vote

Election Day is **Tuesday, November 3, 2026**. Official ballot drop boxes close at **8 p.m.** that day. A mailed ballot must be postmarked by November 3, but the Postal Service recommended mailing by October 27, so a drop box is the safer option now.[^1] King County lists a drop box at Newcastle City Hall, 12835 Newcastle Way.[^11]

Eligible voters can still register or update their information and vote in person by 8 p.m. on Election Day. Voting center hours vary.[^1] Use [VoteWA](https://voter.votewa.gov/) to check your registration and ballot status, find a drop box, and view your personalized guide.[^1]

The clearest lines on this ballot are the three initiatives, the Supreme Court race for Position 5, and the two legislative races, where one candidate in each has a documented record and the other does not. Recommendations in the other races follow the endorsers and are labeled that way. Whatever you decide, return your ballot.
