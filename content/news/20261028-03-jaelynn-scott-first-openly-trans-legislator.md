:Dateline If Jaelynn Scott wins on November 3, she will become Washington's first openly transgender legislator.[^1] Scott, executive director of the **Lavender Rights Project**, took 88.3% of the vote in the August 4 primary for a House seat in Seattle's 37th Legislative District.[^2] Newcastle voters will not see her name on their ballots, because the city is in the 41st Legislative District,[^3] but the Legislature she would join writes laws that apply here too.

## A first for Washington

The Washington State Standard and KNKX both describe Scott as set to become the state's first openly trans lawmaker.[^1][^4] Scott told the Standard she feels being the first is a "heavy burden." She added: "I don't want to do it alone for long, and so I need people to join me."[^1] She also said, "I think it's important that people see themselves in me, and the fact that they can run and they can win."[^1]

Scott is running as a Democratic Socialist.[^4] The Standard reported that she could be one of only two members of the Democratic Socialists of America in the Legislature, along with Rep. Shaun Scott of the 43rd District, who is not related to her.[^1] Shaun Scott told the Standard he hopes her presence on the House floor can "change the ways issues related to the LGBTQ+ and the trans community are discussed."[^1] The Standard noted that some recent legislative debates over parental rights have spiraled into diatribes about transgender people from conservative lawmakers.[^1]

## Who she is

Scott grew up in Mississippi and is an ordained Buddhist minister who no longer practices.[^1] She earned a Master of Divinity in Boulder, Colorado.[^5] She leads the Lavender Rights Project, which the Standard describes as a Black trans advocacy organization,[^1] and this is her first run for office.[^4] She moved to the Central District in late 2024 and has worked in the district for nearly a decade.[^1]

Her organization and Chief Seattle Club are the partners behind Sharyn Grayson House, a 35-unit Capitol Hill building that provides supportive housing for queer, transgender, two-spirit, Black, Indigenous, and other people of color experiencing chronic homelessness through King County's Health Through Housing initiative.[^6]

## The race

Scott's opponent is **Evon McCorkle**, a college student running as an independent.[^1] In the primary, Scott received 33,678 votes and McCorkle 4,143.[^2] The seat is open because Rep. Chipalo Street is seeking the Senate seat Sen. Rebecca Saldaña is leaving to run for the King County Council.[^1]

The money gap is wide. Public Disclosure Commission records viewed on October 28 show Scott's campaign has raised $155,579.32 and spent $116,119.52,[^7] while McCorkle's has raised $750 and spent $23.12.[^8]

## What she says she would do

Scott's top focus is raising more revenue through taxes, which she wants spent on housing construction, education, and health care, along with more publicly owned housing.[^1] She told KNKX the state should "raise more progressive revenue sooner," supports a tax on households earning more than $1 million a year, and would support the state backfilling federal Medicaid cuts.[^4] She has also backed legislation that would bar Washington law enforcement from employing former federal immigration agents hired under President Trump.[^1] She says the Legislature's approach has been "incrementalist" and "too moderate."[^1]

Scott opposes two statewide initiatives on the November ballot, one that would bar transgender girls from girls' school sports and one that would give parents authority to review classroom materials and obtain their children's medical records.[^4] She spoke at the rally launching the "No Hate in WA State" campaign against the Let's Go Washington initiative on girls' sports.[^1] KNKX reported that she says the measures aim to make scapegoats of trans people to start a culture war and distract voters. "We will not be distracted," she said.[^4] Our [October 28 article](/news/2026/10/28/washington-election-transgender-rights) explains what the initiatives would do and who is behind them.

## What happens next

Election Day is November 3. King County's canvassing board meets on November 12, 18, and 23, and the county certifies the results on November 24 at 3 p.m.[^9] The outcome is not final until then, and we will update this story once results are certified.

## What it means in Newcastle

Newcastle residents cannot vote in her race. The city's state legislators come from the 41st District,[^3] and our [Newcastle voter's guide](/news/2026/10/28/newcastle-voters-guide) covers the races on local ballots. An openly trans legislator in Olympia would still matter to trans neighbors here. The Legislature passes laws for every Washington community, and for the first time, one of its members would speak from lived experience as an openly trans person.
