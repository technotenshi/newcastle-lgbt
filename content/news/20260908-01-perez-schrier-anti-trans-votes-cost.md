:Dateline U.S. Reps. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (D-WA-03) and Kim Schrier (D-WA-08) voted in September 2025 to bar transgender women at the nation's military academies from women's sports teams, then voted in December 2025 for the defense bill that made that ban federal law.[^1][^2][^3] They are Washington's two entries in Erin Reed's national guide to congressional Democrats with anti-trans voting records.[^4] Perez went further. In May 2026 she voted for a bill that would make public elementary and middle schools get a parent's consent before changing a student's name or pronouns on school forms, which the Congressional Equality Caucus calls forced outing.[^5][^6][^7]

## Both Voted to Bar Trans Women From Military Academy Teams

On September 10, 2025, during House debate on the fiscal year 2026 National Defense Authorization Act (H.R. 3838), Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC) offered Part A Amendment No. 15. It bars the service academies (West Point, Annapolis and the Air Force Academy)[^8] from letting a cadet or midshipman the amendment defines as male compete on a women's team.[^9] It passed 227 to 201. Perez and Schrier were two of just 10 House Democrats who voted Aye, joining 217 Republicans.[^1]

Mace was open about whom the amendment targets. "This amendment ends the nonsense, protects fairness, and ensures female athletes at our nation's military academies are not forced to give up opportunities they've worked hard to earn," she said the day before the vote, in a press release about keeping "men" out of women's sports.[^10] The people she calls men are transgender women training to become U.S. military officers. Perez and Schrier voted for her amendment.

It was one of six anti-trans amendments Republicans attached to the bill that week.[^11] Perez and Schrier voted No on four of them: Rep. Ralph Norman's amendment barring gender transition services (No. 13),[^12] Mace's amendment restricting TRICARE coverage of gender-affirming care (No. 14),[^13] the amendment removing gender identity from military surveys (No. 16)[^14] and the amendment limiting military single-sex spaces to what it calls "biological sex" (No. 17).[^15] On Rep. Cory Mills's amendment restricting the flags flown at Defense Department workplaces (No. 18), which Erin in the Morning's s. baum calls a functional ban on Pride flags, Perez voted Aye and Schrier voted No.[^16][^11]

## The Ban Became Law, and Both Voted for It

The House passed its version of the bill the same day, 231 to 196. Perez voted for it. Schrier voted against it.[^17] The House and Senate then negotiated a final FY2026 NDAA (S. 1071). The House passed it on December 10, 2025, 312 to 112, with both Perez and Schrier voting Yea.[^2] The Senate agreed 77 to 20 on December 17, and the bill became Public Law 119-60 on December 18.[^18]

Section 559A of that law directs the Secretary of Defense to keep anyone "whose sex is male," defined by reproductive biology and genetics at birth, out of women's and girls' athletic programs at the Military, Naval and Air Force academies. Males may train or practice with those teams only if no woman loses a roster spot, a competition opportunity, a scholarship or another benefit.[^3] None of the other five September amendments appears in the enacted text.[^3] The sports ban is the only one of the six that became law, and it is the one both Washington Democrats voted for.

The final bill was a large defense package, and 115 House Democrats voted for it.[^2] That is context, not an excuse. Schrier voted against the House version in September and still voted for the final bill that carried the ban into law. Perez voted for both.

## What Exclusion Costs Trans People

Shutting trans people out of shared spaces has a measurable cost. A 2019 peer-reviewed study in *Pediatrics* of 3,673 transgender and nonbinary students in grades 7 through 12 found that those whose schools restricted restroom and locker-room use were more likely to be sexually assaulted. For transgender girls the risk ratio was 2.49.[^19] The study measured school facility rules, not athletics, but it documents what exclusion costs the people excluded. The Trevor Project's 2025 national survey of more than 16,000 LGBTQ+ young people found that transgender and nonbinary youth with access to gender-neutral school bathrooms, clothing that matched their gender and pronouns respected by the people they live with reported lower rates of attempting suicide.[^20]

The academy ban directly affects few people, which makes it easy to describe as a minor compromise. The evidence says exclusion is not minor for the people it targets.

## They Voted Against Nationwide Bans, Then for This One

Perez and Schrier voted against both nationwide sports bans the House has passed. In April 2023, H.R. 734, which would have barred transgender girls and women from K-12 and college sports under Title IX, passed 219 to 203 on a near party-line vote, with both voting No.[^21] In January 2025 its successor, H.R. 28, passed 218 to 206, and both voted No again.[^22] Neither became law; the Senate took no action on either.[^23][^24]

Those No votes matter. The academy amendment applied the same exclusion to three schools instead of every school, and both voted for it. A narrower ban is still a ban for the trans women it covers.

## Perez Voted for a Bill That Would Force Schools to Out Young Trans Students

On May 20, 2026, the House passed H.R. 2616, the "Stopping Indoctrination and Protecting Kids Act," 217 to 198. Perez was one of eight House Democrats who voted for it. Schrier voted against it.[^5][^25] As a condition of federal education funding, the bill requires public elementary and middle schools to get a parent's consent before changing a student's "gender markers, pronouns, or preferred name on any school form" or their "sex-based accommodations, including locker rooms or bathrooms." It has no exception for a child whose home is not safe. A separate section bars those federal funds from being used "to teach or advance concepts related to gender ideology," a term the bill takes from President Trump's Executive Order 14168.[^6] The Senate referred the bill to its Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions Committee on May 21, 2026, and it has not become law.[^26]

Perez defended her vote two days later: "This bill requires schools to get a parent's consent before changing an elementary or middle school student's pronouns or name on school forms. My heart breaks for children who don't feel safe letting their parents into their world. But divorcing a young child's school life from their home life doesn't resolve the underlying problem of an unsupportive family. It can leave that child more alone."[^27] Her statement leaves out two parts of the bill she voted for. The consent rule also covers bathrooms and locker rooms, and the bill bars federal funds from teaching anything that falls under the executive order's definition of "gender ideology."[^6][^27]

The Congressional Equality Caucus called H.R. 2616 a "forced outing" bill that would require schools to out transgender students "even if doing so would put the student in immediate danger." Its chair, Rep. Mark Takano (D-CA), said: "Republicans claim to be the party of small government, but they have no problem bringing the full force of the federal government down against children."[^7]

## Outing a Child to an Unsupportive Family Has a Documented Cost

Perez's own statement concedes that some children do not feel safe at home. The research on family rejection shows what is at stake for them. A 2009 *Pediatrics* study of 224 white and Latino lesbian, gay and bisexual young adults found that those who reported high levels of family rejection as teenagers were 8.4 times more likely to have attempted suicide, and 5.9 times more likely to report high levels of depression, than those who reported little or none.[^28] The Trevor Project's 2025 survey found that LGBTQ+ young people in very accepting communities attempted suicide at less than a third the rate of those in very unaccepting ones.[^20]

Under H.R. 2616, a school that knows a child is at risk at home must still go to the parents before changing that child's name or pronouns on school forms, or their bathroom or locker-room access. Bringing families in works when the family is safe. The bill gives schools no way to protect a child when it is not.

## "We Miss Some of the Nuance"

Reed's guide also cites Perez's criticism of how Democrats defend trans athletes.[^4] On CNN on July 1, 2026, the day after the U.S. Supreme Court upheld state bans on transgender girls in girls' sports, Perez described town halls with parents who had spent years driving daughters to practice in hopes of a scholarship. "And so when we rush to moralize and be like, this is all about love versus hate, I think we miss some of the nuance that people are seeing in this," she said. "I think we can put up walls where there don't necessarily need to be them."[^29]

The ruling she was answering, *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* and a companion Idaho case, upheld both states' bans. The justices were unanimous that the laws do not violate federal civil rights law; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson dissented on whether West Virginia's law is constitutional as applied to the athlete in that case.[^30] The ruling allowed states to ban trans girls from girls' teams. It did not require any state to.

Removing a group of girls from their teams is not a matter of nuance. It is an exclusion with a documented cost, and when a Democrat calls the defense of those girls a "rush to moralize," she hands that argument to the people writing the bans.

## Washington's Protections Hold, and I-638 Targets Them

When the ruling came down, Washington's leaders said it changed nothing here. State Superintendent Chris Reykdal said it "reinforces Washington state's ability to adopt and implement our own laws in alignment with our values,"[^31] and Attorney General Nick Brown said, "This ruling does not change Washington law."[^32] The Washington Interscholastic Activities Association adopted a model policy on transgender athletes in 2007,[^33] and its handbook lets students compete "consistent with their gender identity or the gender most consistently expressed." Of roughly 250,000 student-athletes statewide, five to ten are transgender.[^32]

Those protections are now on the November ballot. I-638 would bar students its text calls "biologically male" from girls' school competitions and require sex verification for students who enter them.[^34] We oppose it. Our [earlier coverage](/news/2026/09/04/anti-lgbtq-ballot-initiatives-documented-risks) documents the campaign behind it, and our [reporting on the federal Medicaid rule](/news/2026/09/06/federal-assault-transgender-youth-healthcare) covers the medical evidence on gender-affirming care.

H.R. 2616 is the federal threat still pending. If the Senate passed it and the president signed it, its consent rule would reach every public elementary and middle school in the state that receives federal education funds, including the schools that serve Newcastle.[^6][^26]

## What This Record Says

Both representatives voted to exclude trans women from their teams, then voted for the bill that made the exclusion law. Both also voted against the broader bans and against most of the September amendments, so a No vote was plainly available to them on the one that became law.

Perez's record goes further: the flags amendment, the House NDAA, H.R. 2616 and a national TV interview telling Democrats they "rush to moralize" on trans athletes. Her stated reason for H.R. 2616 is to bring families in, but the bill she voted for has no protection for the children her own statement says are not safe at home. Schrier's record is narrower, but her two votes are on the only anti-trans provision from that week that is now law.

On November 3, Perez faces Republican John Braun and Schrier faces Republican Spencer Meline.[^35][^36] Facing a worse opponent is not approval of this record. Perez and Schrier represent Washington's trans constituents too, and these votes should cost them constituent pressure now and a serious primary challenge in 2028 from someone who would not cast them.

## What a 2028 Primary Challenge Looks Like

In WA-03, progressive Democrat Brent Hennrich ran to Perez's left in the August 2026 primary and finished third with 16.7% of the vote, behind Republican John Braun's 39.7% and Perez's 36.4%.[^35] Hennrich's platform commits to supporting "LGBTQ+ anti-discrimination legislation and any other anti-discrimination language at the Federal level,"[^37] and Progressive Victory, a national group that backs progressive candidates, lists him among the ones it says it "loves."[^38] After the primary, Hennrich put the burden on Perez: "I believe the onus is on MGP to earn every vote. She needs to reach out to those who feel harmed by her voting record and work with them to find a path to earning their support."[^39] His platform does not address the academy ban or H.R. 2616 by name. Ask him, and every 2028 candidate in both districts, to commit to voting No on both.

WA-08 had no comparable option this cycle. Democrat Keith Arnold challenged Schrier in the primary and did not finish in the top three, behind Schrier at 53.8% and Republicans Spencer Meline at 15.9% and Trinh Ha at 14.8%.[^36] iVoterGuide asked Arnold whether biological males should take part in women's sports or use women's spaces, and whether marriage is a union of one man and one woman, and it records "Did not answer" for both.[^40] The district has no documented pro-trans alternative waiting for 2028. Building one starts with constituent pressure now.
